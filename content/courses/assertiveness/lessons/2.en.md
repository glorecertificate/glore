---
title: What assertiveness means
---

This is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to express one's own point of view while respecting and taking into consideration the views of others; and to share and accept constructive criticism, different opinions and advice.

Being assertive sits between two habits that most people know well. A passive person keeps quiet and gives in. An aggressive person pushes their view and ignores everyone else. An assertive person says what they think and listens to the answer.

## Expressing your own point of view

This starts with knowing what you think and what you need. Then you say it directly, with "I" statements: "I need a break after two hours" instead of "You never let anybody rest." You stay calm, you keep your voice steady and you do not apologise for having an opinion.

In a volunteer meeting, the team plans a festival stall. You think the sign-up queue will block the entrance. You say, "I'm worried the queue will block the entrance. Could we move the table to the side?" That is clear, short and about the problem, not about a person.

## Respecting the views of others

Assertiveness is not winning. You listen to the other view and you take it seriously, even if you disagree. You can say, "I see why you prefer the morning session. For me the afternoon works better. What can we do?"

At university, a classmate wants to split the group project in a way that gives you the writing. You say that you would prefer to present, and you ask what the others want. You may end up trading, and that is a good result.

## Sharing and accepting criticism, opinions and advice

Sharing criticism means describing the issue and its effect, not attacking the person. Accepting it means listening without interrupting, asking for details and then deciding what is useful. You can thank someone for advice and still choose not to follow it.

At work, your supervisor suggests you speak less during client calls. You ask for an example and realise you often interrupt. You decide to try it, and you also ask for the same honesty in return.

## Saying no

A short, kind no is part of assertiveness: "Thank you for asking. I can't do it this time." You do not need a long excuse. If you can offer an alternative, you can, but you do not have to.
