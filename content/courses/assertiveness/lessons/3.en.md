title: Practice
questions:
  - question: Your team leader at a refugee welcome centre asks you to stay two extra hours, for the third time this week. You are tired and have a class tomorrow. Which reply is assertive?
    options:
      - text: Fine, I'll stay. It's not like I have a choice.
      - text: No. Stop asking me, I already do too much.
      - text: I want to help, but I can't stay tonight because of class tomorrow. I could stay for one extra hour on Friday. Would that work?
        correct: true
    explanation: The first reply is passive and hides the real feeling behind sarcasm. The second is aggressive and turns the request into an attack. The third states a limit, gives the reason and offers a realistic alternative, so both people's needs are on the table.
  - question: In a group project, a classmate says your idea won't work and suggests a different one. You feel hurt. What is the most assertive first move?
    options:
      - text: Insist that your idea is better and explain it again, louder.
      - text: Agree to their idea at once so that there is no conflict.
      - text: Ask what worries them about your idea, listen to the answer, then say what you think of theirs.
        correct: true
    explanation: Asking and listening lets you see whether the criticism is useful before you react. Insisting treats the disagreement as a fight, and giving in at once means your view is never heard.
  - question: A volunteer coordinator tells you, in front of the group, that you talk too much in meetings. Which response best shows that you can accept criticism?
    options:
      - text: Thank you for telling me. Could you give me an example from today so I can see what you mean? Then we can talk about it afterwards.
        correct: true
      - text: That's unfair. Nobody else gets told this.
      - text: Sorry, sorry, I'll never speak again.
      - text: Laugh it off and carry on as before.
    explanation: Asking for an example turns a vague comment into something you can act on, and suggesting a private follow-up respects both of you. Defending yourself, over-apologising or ignoring the comment each stop the feedback from doing any good.
  - question: You disagree with a decision your team has just made about the schedule of an online campaign. Which sentence expresses your view and respects the team?
    options:
      - text: Whatever, do what you want.
      - text: I see the reason for posting at night. I'm worried that our audience will not be online. Could we test one week at 18:00 and compare?
        correct: true
      - text: That is a bad decision and everyone knows it.
    explanation: The strong answer recognises the logic of the decision, states a concern with a reason and proposes a way to check. The other two close the conversation, one by withdrawing and the other by attacking.

Read these situations and decide how you would react.

At an elderly care home, a resident's daughter tells you that you are doing the activity wrong. You think you are following the plan.

In a youth exchange, a new friend keeps joking about your accent. You smile each time, but it bothers you.

At work, a colleague asks to borrow your notes for the third time without reading the shared folder.
