---
title: The fallen tree on the trail
---

Kasper volunteers at a nature reserve on the west coast of Denmark. His coordinator, Birte, has gone to a training course for a week and has little phone signal. Before leaving she gave him a short list: walk the three trails, replace the broken signs and count the nesting boxes.

On Tuesday, after a night of strong wind, Kasper finds a large tree across the north trail. Nobody has told him what to do about it. He takes a photo and writes down the spot. Clearing a tree of that size needs a chainsaw and training, so he does not try it. He ties red tape across the trail on both sides and puts up a sign pointing to the shorter loop. Then he leaves a message for Birte with the photo, the location and what he did, so she can decide how to remove it when she is back.

He goes on with the rest of the list and, since he finishes the signs early, he starts on the nesting boxes in the south part, which were the next item. On Friday Birte reads his message and replies with one line: "Thanks, that is exactly what I would have done."

Think about your own experience:

- Think of a time when you had to work with no one checking on you. What helped you keep going?
- When did you wait for instructions that you could have worked out yourself? What would you do differently?
- When was it right to ask before acting? How did you know?
