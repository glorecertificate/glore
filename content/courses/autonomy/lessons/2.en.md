---
title: What autonomy means
---

This is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to work independently and proactively without constant outside monitoring and instruction.

Autonomy has three parts: you work independently, you are proactive, and you need little monitoring or instruction. Asking questions is still part of it.

## Working independently

Working independently means you can take a task from start to finish with the information you were given. You understand the goal and the limits (time, budget, what you may decide), and then you plan your own steps. If something is unclear, you first try to find the answer: you read the guide, check earlier examples or ask a colleague who is nearby.

At a youth centre, a volunteer is asked to prepare the room for a film night. She does not wait to be told where the chairs go. She remembers last month's layout, checks the projector the day before and writes a short list of what is missing.

## Being proactive

Proactive people look ahead. When your task is done, you ask what is next and you look at the agreed priorities to find it. When you see a problem coming, you act or warn people while there is time to fix it.

In a university group, a student sees that the shared folder is a mess and that the deadline for the final report is close. Nobody has asked her to tidy it. She spends twenty minutes giving the files clear names and posts a short message. Her teammates find the documents easily and nobody has to chase them.

## Without constant monitoring and instruction

Trust grows when people do what they said without being reminded. In practice this means you track your own progress, you tell the people involved how it is going, and you share the result when it is ready. A short update at the end of the day or the week is enough for most coordinators.

Autonomy also includes knowing where your limits are. Some decisions are yours: which route to take for the trail walk, how to arrange the tables. Others affect money, safety, other people's work or the reputation of the organization, and those call for a quick check. In the example of the fallen tree, Kasper acted on what was safe and left the rest for his coordinator. Asking at the right moment is part of working independently.

At work, a new employee is given a week-long project and a deadline. She breaks it into steps, starts on Monday and sends a two-line update on Wednesday. Her manager does not need to check in, and she asks one question about the budget before she spends anything.
