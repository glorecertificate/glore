---
title: Practice
questions:
  - question: Your coordinator left you a weekly task list at the animal shelter, and you finish it by Wednesday. What do you do?
    options:
      - text: Check the agreed priorities, start the next useful task and tell your coordinator at the next check-in.
        correct: true
      - text: Wait until the coordinator gives you new tasks.
      - text: Go home early, since the list is done.
      - text: Reorganise the whole storage room in your own way.
    explanation: Proactive people use the goals they were given to find the next step, and they keep the coordinator informed. Waiting wastes time that could help. Changing something large without agreement goes beyond what you were asked to decide.
  - question: You do not know how the new booking tool works, and your coordinator is away for two days. What is the best first step?
    options:
      - text: Do nothing until the coordinator is back.
      - text: Book people in a spreadsheet of your own and ignore the tool.
      - text: Ask every volunteer in the group chat at once.
      - text: Look for a guide, try the tool on a test entry, and write down the questions that remain.
        correct: true
    explanation: Looking for answers yourself is part of working independently, and writing down the remaining questions makes the conversation with your coordinator short and useful. Waiting stops the work, and a separate system creates problems for everyone else.
  - question: While preparing a trip for young people, you realise a good activity would cost 150 euros that are not in your budget. What do you do?
    options:
      - text: Pay for it and mention it afterwards.
      - text: Ask your coordinator before you spend, and say what the activity adds.
        correct: true
      - text: Cancel the idea without telling anyone.
      - text: Ask each participant to contribute money.
    explanation: Autonomy means knowing which decisions are yours. Spending money outside the budget is not one of them, so a quick question is the independent move here. Spending first, dropping a good idea silently, or collecting money from participants all skip the check.
  - question: You sent participants the wrong start time for tomorrow's workshop. Your coordinator has not noticed. What do you do?
    options:
      - text: Hope that people read the programme and notice by themselves.
      - text: Wait for your coordinator to find the mistake.
      - text: Send a correction now, then tell your coordinator what happened and how you fixed it.
        correct: true
      - text: Delete the message and say nothing.
    explanation: Taking responsibility without being asked is part of working without monitoring. A quick correction limits the damage, and telling your coordinator keeps trust. Hoping or hiding leaves participants confused and your coordinator surprised.
  - question: You are studying online for a certificate, and nobody checks your progress. The exam is in two weeks. What helps you most?
    options:
      - text: Set weekly goals, note when you meet them and adjust if you fall behind.
        correct: true
      - text: Study when you feel like it and see how far you get.
      - text: Ask a friend to remind you every day.
    explanation: Without outside monitoring, you take over the monitoring yourself. Weekly goals and a record of progress show early when you need to change the plan. Waiting for motivation or depending on reminders puts the control back outside you.
---

Read these situations and think about how you would act on your own.

At a summer festival, your shift leader is called away for an hour and nobody told you what to do when the queue gets long.

In an online campaign, you are asked to write posts for the next month. The coordinator is busy and gave you only a topic.

At work, your manager is on holiday, and a customer asks for something you have never handled before.
