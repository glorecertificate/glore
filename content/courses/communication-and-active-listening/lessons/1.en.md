---
title: A volunteer who says "fine"
---

Lena is spending six months as a volunteer at a youth centre in Gdansk. On Thursday afternoons she runs the homework corner. This week a fifteen-year-old called Karim sits down, opens his notebook and does not write anything.

"How's it going?" Lena asks.

"Fine," he says, looking at the table. His shoulders are pulled up and his foot is tapping.

Lena has a stack of worksheets to hand out and a coordinator waiting for her in ten minutes. She could say "Great!" and move on. Instead she puts the worksheets down, sits next to him and waits a few seconds.

"You look like something is bothering you. Do you want to tell me, or would you rather just work for a bit?"

Karim shrugs. After a pause he says that his teacher gave him a low mark, and he thinks everyone in the class now believes he is stupid. Lena does not rush to fix it. She asks what exactly the teacher wrote, and what Karim thinks he could do next time. By the end of the hour he has opened the maths book.

Think about a moment when you were in Lena's place, or in Karim's:

- Has someone said "fine" or "no problem" while their face or voice said something else? What did you notice, and what did you do?
- When did a person ask you the right question at the right moment? What made it the right one?
- When was the last time you only waited for your turn to speak instead of listening?
