---
title: What communication and active listening mean
---

Here is how GloRe defines the skill:

> Ability to understand both verbal and non-verbal messages, and to ask questions to better understand the situation. This also includes the ability to answer questions, and to give and to receive constructive feedback.

The definition has four parts.

## Understanding verbal and non-verbal messages

People communicate with words, but also with tone, pace, eye contact, posture and silence. Active listening means giving your full attention to all of it. You put your phone away, you do not plan your answer while the other person is talking, and you check what you understood: "So you are worried the group will not finish on time?"

At a beach clean-up, a volunteer says "I'm happy to carry the bags" in a flat voice and avoids your eyes. The words say yes. The rest may say that the bags are too heavy for them.

## Asking questions

Open questions start with what, how or tell me about, and they invite a longer answer. "How did the interview go?" gets you more than "Was it good?" Good questions are curious, not suspicious, and they come after you have listened to the first answer.

In a study group, a classmate says the essay is "a mess". Instead of offering a fix, you ask: "Which part feels messy to you?" Now you both know where to start.

## Answering questions

Listening is only half of a conversation. A clear answer addresses the question that was asked, uses simple words and says "I don't know, but I will find out" when that is the truth. This matters most when the person asking is new, nervous or speaking a second language.

## Giving and receiving constructive feedback

Constructive feedback is specific, kind and about something the person can change. "The sign-in table was crowded because the queue started inside the door" is useful. "You organised it badly" is not.

Receiving feedback is a skill too. You listen to the end, you ask for an example if you need one, and you say thank you before you decide what to do with it.

At work, a manager tells you that your emails are hard to follow. You ask, "Can you show me one that was unclear?" and next week you start each message with the main request. That is the whole cycle: listen, ask, answer, improve.
