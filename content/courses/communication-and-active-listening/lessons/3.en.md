---
title: Practice
questions:
  - question: Daniel, a festival volunteer, asks a new colleague if the shift schedule is clear. She says "Yes, yes" but keeps looking at her phone and frowns. What is the best next step?
    options:
      - text: Take her word for it and move on, since she said yes.
      - text: Say that you noticed she seemed unsure, and ask which part of the schedule she would like to go through again.
        correct: true
      - text: Repeat the whole schedule slowly and more loudly.
    explanation: Her words and her body say different things, so there is probably a doubt she has not voiced. Naming what you noticed without blaming her, and asking about a specific part, makes it easy to speak. Repeating everything louder does not address her real question.
  - question: Which question is most likely to help you understand how a refugee support session went for a participant?
    options:
      - text: Did you like the session?
      - text: Don't you think the session was too long?
      - text: What was the most useful part of the session for you, and what would you change?
        correct: true
    explanation: Open questions invite a fuller answer. The first question can be answered with a single word, and the second pushes the person towards your own opinion.
  - question: A teammate tells you that your presentation slides had too much text. What is the best first reaction?
    options:
      - text: Explain why you made the slides that way.
      - text: Say that other people liked the slides.
      - text: Agree with everything and say nothing more.
      - text: Listen to the end, ask which slide was the hardest to read, and thank them.
        correct: true
    explanation: Feedback is information. Listening and asking for one example helps you learn what to change, and thanking the person keeps the door open for future feedback. Defending yourself closes it, and agreeing silently shows you did not engage with it.
  - question: You want to tell a volunteer that she often arrives ten minutes late to the elderly care visits. Which feedback is the most constructive?
    options:
      - text: The last three visits started at 15:10 instead of 15:00, and Mr Rossi was waiting at the door. Could we find a way to help you arrive on time?
        correct: true
      - text: You are never on time and it is annoying.
      - text: Maybe next time try to be a bit more responsible.
    explanation: Good feedback describes what happened with concrete facts, mentions the effect on a person, and moves towards a solution. Labels such as "never" or "irresponsible" attack the person and give no clear thing to change.

Read the three situations and think about what you would say. Then answer the questions below.

At the youth centre. A boy who usually jokes a lot is quiet during the football game. He says he is just tired.

In a study group. A classmate asks you, "Did you understand the instructions for the project?" You are not sure you did.

At work. Your supervisor says, "That report was OK," and moves to another topic.
