---
title: The first time with the whistle
---

Dana volunteers at a youth football club in Cluj. The coach, Radu, has to leave for a family emergency halfway through Saturday's session and asks Dana to run the last half hour. She has never done it. Her stomach tightens, and the first thing she says is that someone more experienced should do it.

Radu shrugs. "You've been here every week since September. You know the drills." She knows he is right about the drills, so she says yes. During the warm-up, her voice shakes and she forgets the whistle. Twelve-year-old Mihai picks it up from the grass and holds it out without a word.

Halfway through, she sees that two players have drifted away from the game. Instead of doing the activity herself, she asks Mihai, who is the oldest, to lead the small-team match while she watches the others. He grins, a little stunned, and does it well.

On the way home, Dana realises that she got through it by remembering what she already knew, and by letting someone else take a real part. She also sees she did not need to be perfect for anyone to trust her.

Think about your own experience:

- When did you do something you were not sure you could do? What helped you start?
- Who trusts you with something important, and how does that change the way you act?
- When did you trust someone with a task and it went better than you expected? What if it had not?
