---
title: What confidence in oneself and others means
---

This is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to believe in oneself and others, and to establish trusting relationships.

Confidence is believing that you can handle what comes, including mistakes, and extending the same belief to the people around you. Trusting relationships grow from that.

## Believing in oneself

Self-belief is built on evidence. Think about what you have already managed: a project you finished, a difficult conversation you got through, a skill you picked up from scratch. When a new task feels too big, those memories remind you that you have coped before.

It also grows by trying. You take on a task slightly beyond your comfort zone, you do it imperfectly, and you notice that you survived. Each time, the next step feels a little smaller. Mistakes become information you can use.

A volunteer at a refugee support centre is asked to give a welcome talk to new arrivals. She says she is not ready. She remembers the three groups she has already guided around the centre, writes her notes for the talk, and gives it. It is not perfect, and two people thank her afterwards.

Confidence includes saying "I have not done this before, and I can learn it," and asking for help without feeling smaller.

## Believing in others

Believing in someone means assuming they can do something until you see otherwise, and giving them a chance to show it. In a group, this can be as simple as handing a newer teammate a real task instead of a trivial one.

At a student association, the treasurer is nervous about passing the budget to a first-year who offered to help. She explains the task, agrees on a date for the first check-in, and then leaves the work to him. He makes one error, they fix it together, and he handles the rest well.

When you trust others, match the responsibility to the person and the situation, stay available, and accept that people will not do things exactly your way.

## Establishing trusting relationships

Trust builds slowly and breaks quickly. It comes from small, repeated actions: arriving when you said you would, doing what you promised, being honest when something is going wrong, and keeping private things private.

When you make a mistake, say so early and say what you will do about it. Most people can forgive an honest error. They lose trust when they find out later that something was hidden.

In a new workplace, a young colleague tells her manager that she misread a deadline and the report will be a day late. She proposes a quick plan to catch up. Her manager is annoyed but also notes that she spoke up at once, and next time hands her a bigger task.

## Both directions

Trust in yourself and trust in others support one another. People who feel secure in their own abilities can let others take part without feeling threatened, and people who are trusted tend to grow more sure of themselves.
