title: Practice
questions:
  - question: In a team meeting someone dismisses your idea and you feel your anger rising. What is the best move?
    options:
      - text: Raise your voice until they hear you.
      - text: Stay silent and avoid speaking to that person afterwards.
      - text: Say you need a short break, breathe, and come back to the conversation.
        correct: true
    explanation: Noticing your emotion and giving yourself a pause protects the conversation. Raising your voice escalates it, and avoiding the person leaves the problem unsolved while making it harder to work together.
  - question: Two volunteers both want the Saturday slot at the information stall. What gives the best chance of a fair outcome?
    options:
      - text: Ask each one what matters about that slot, then look for an option that covers both, for example sharing the slot or alternating weeks.
        correct: true
      - text: Give the slot to whoever has volunteered for longer.
      - text: Ask the coordinator to decide without speaking to them first.
      - text: Let the one who looks more upset have it.
    explanation: Asking about interests often reveals that the needs are not the same, such as one person needing the morning for family reasons. Then more than one solution is possible. The other options decide who wins without looking at what each person needs.
  - question: A study group has a deadline. One member is late, and the group chat fills with comments about how lazy he is. What should you do?
    options:
      - text: Add a list of all the times he has been late.
      - text: Leave the chat and let the others deal with it.
      - text: Say nothing and quietly do his part.
      - text: Write to him privately, say you are worried about the deadline, and ask what is making it hard.
        correct: true
    explanation: A private, curious message treats the problem as a shared one and gives him a chance to explain, perhaps something you do not know. Public blame makes people defensive. Leaving or doing his part quietly avoids the conversation the group needs.
  - question: A fellow volunteer keeps making comments about your body, and he did not stop after you asked him to. What is the right step?
    options:
      - text: Wait for it to stop, because confronting him could make things worse.
      - text: Tell the coordinator or the person responsible for safeguarding, so that it is dealt with by someone who can act.
        correct: true
      - text: Try to settle it in a private conversation until he agrees.
      - text: Laugh it off, so he sees it does not bother you.
    explanation: Harassment is not a normal conflict, and you should never have to resolve it alone or face the person on your own. Reporting it protects you and others. Waiting or laughing can allow it to continue, and a private confrontation may be unsafe.
  - question: Two flatmates disagree about noise. One studies at night, the other starts work at six. Which agreement meets both needs?
    options:
      - text: Quiet hours from ten at night, headphones for music, and a short note when a late evening is exceptional.
        correct: true
      - text: Whoever complains first gets what they want.
      - text: Take turns each week, and the person whose week it is does what they like.
      - text: One of them looks for a new place to live.
    explanation: The first agreement protects sleep and study time and leaves room for exceptions, which suits both. A rule where the loudest wins, or one that gives total freedom half the time, leaves one need unmet each time.
---

Read these situations and think about how you would handle them.

At a youth camp, two teenagers argue about which game to play, and both turn to you.

In a group project, a teammate rewrote your section without telling you.

At work, a colleague rolls their eyes whenever you speak in meetings.
