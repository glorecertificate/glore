title: Practice
questions:
  - question: Your sports club wants more families to come to the open day. The usual flyers have brought few people for three years. What is the best creative first step?
    options:
      - text: Print the flyers in brighter colours and hand out more of them.
      - text: Write down five or six different ways to reach families, including unusual ones, before choosing which to try.
        correct: true
      - text: Copy exactly what the biggest club in town does.
    explanation: Listing several options before choosing opens up possibilities the group has not considered. Brighter flyers repeat the same approach with a small tweak, and copying another club gives you their solution to their situation, not yours.
  - question: At a language café, the same few people talk and newcomers stay silent. You have an idea, but it means changing a rule that has always been in place, that everyone talks in one big circle. What do you do?
    options:
      - text: Keep the circle, because changing established rules causes trouble.
      - text: Wait until someone else proposes a change.
      - text: Suggest trying small groups for the first twenty minutes next week, then ask people how it felt.
        correct: true
    explanation: Questioning the rule is thinking outside the box, and proposing a short, low-risk trial turns the idea into something people can judge from experience. Keeping everything as is or waiting for someone else leaves the problem as it was.
  - question: A teammate suggests an idea you think will not work. Which response keeps the creative process going?
    options:
      - text: Ask what problem the idea would solve, and what a version of it could look like on a small scale.
        correct: true
      - text: Tell them it has been tried before and failed.
      - text: Smile and say nothing, then do it your way.
      - text: Point out three reasons it will not work.
    explanation: Questions help you understand the idea and may reveal a workable core. Dismissing it, quietly ignoring it, or listing the reasons it fails ends the conversation and teaches the person to stop sharing ideas.
  - question: A volunteer team at an elderly care home notices that the weekly activity sign-up sheet is often lost. You propose a new way. How do you test it?
    options:
      - text: Announce the new way for the whole home and start next week.
      - text: Replace the sheet immediately, then ask how it went after a month.
      - text: Wait for the care home manager to design a better system.
      - text: Try it for two weeks with one floor, ask residents and staff what worked, then adjust.
        correct: true
    explanation: A small trial limits the cost if the idea needs changes, and feedback from the people using it tells you what to fix. Launching everywhere at once risks upsetting a routine that residents depend on, and waiting leaves the problem unsolved.
  - question: In an online campaign for a charity, engagement has dropped. Which idea shows the most useful kind of innovation?
    options:
      - text: Post at the same times every day, just more often.
      - text: Invite followers to send short videos about why they volunteer, share the best ones, and see whether engagement changes.
        correct: true
      - text: Delete older posts so the page looks tidier.
      - text: Buy a more expensive tool for scheduling posts.
    explanation: The second option tries something new that involves the audience, and it is easy to measure. Posting more often repeats the same approach, and the other two change how the page looks or how it is run without addressing why people stopped engaging.

Read these situations and think about how you would respond.

At a refugee support centre, volunteers hand out welcome packs, but many arrivals do not read the printed leaflet inside because it is only in two languages.

In a student association, the monthly meeting runs for two hours and half the members leave early.

At a summer festival, the queue for the volunteer tent grows every year, and the team keeps adding more tables without it getting faster.
