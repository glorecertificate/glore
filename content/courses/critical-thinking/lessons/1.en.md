---
title: The statistic that went viral
---

Jonas is part of the online team of an environmental group in Vilnius. Each week the team posts one fact about waste and recycling. On Monday a teammate shares a graphic from a social media account: "90% of recycled plastic ends up in landfill." It already has thousands of shares, and the team wants to post it before the hashtag disappears.

Jonas feels uneasy. The number is dramatic, which is the reason it spreads. He opens the account's page and finds no source. He searches the figure and finds three versions of it, each with a different percentage and a different year. One comes from a report that talks about plastic in a single country, not recycling in general.

He tells the team, "I don't think we should post this yet. Can we find where it started?" Someone replies that everyone else is posting it. Jonas says that is true, and asks what would happen if the number turned out to be wrong. Another volunteer remembers an organisation that publishes yearly waste data. They post a smaller number with its source and a link, a day later than planned. It gets fewer shares, and two people write to thank them for the link.

Think about your own experience:

- When did you share or believe something that later turned out to be wrong? What made it convincing?
- Whose opinions or news sources do you trust most? Have you ever checked why?
- When did talking to someone with a different view change your mind, or at least your understanding?
