---
title: What critical thinking means
---

This is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to evaluate information and interpret it through independent and unconstrained analysis; and to analyze various situations and think from different perspectives.

Critical thinking is the habit of asking good questions before you accept an idea, and staying curious about other ways to see a situation. It does not require distrusting everybody.

## Evaluating information

Information reaches you from friends, social media, teachers, news and websites. Evaluating it starts with a few simple questions. Who says this, and what do they gain? What is the evidence? Is it recent? Do other reliable sources say the same? Is the headline telling you what the article says, or only trying to make you click?

A message in your volunteering group chat says that a local pool is offering free swimming lessons to refugees. Before you forward it to ten people, you can check the pool's own website or call them. It takes five minutes, and it saves people a trip.

## Independent and unconstrained analysis

Independent means you reach your own conclusion by looking at the facts, not because a group, an influencer or an authority said so. Unconstrained means you let yourself question things, including ideas that are popular or comfortable, and including your own.

This can be difficult in a group, where it feels easier to agree. At university, your study group says a theory is "obviously" right. You ask what the evidence is, and politely check the reading. You may find that they were right, and now you know why. Or you may find a gap.

At work, a manager proposes a new schedule because "that's how it has always been done." You can ask, "What problem is it solving? What happens if we try a different schedule for two weeks?"

## Interpreting and analysing situations

Interpreting means finding the meaning in facts. The same numbers can lead to different stories, so you look for the explanations that fit best and notice where you are guessing. Analysing a situation means breaking it into parts: what happened, who is involved, what causes might exist, what the options are.

At a youth centre, attendance at the Friday workshop has dropped by half. You could blame the participants. If you look further, you notice that a school exam started that week, a bus line changed its timetable, and the event is advertised only in English. Each cause suggests a different action.

## Thinking from different perspectives

Ask how the situation looks to someone with different experience, age, culture or interests. Talk to people who disagree with you and try to explain their view well enough that they would say, "Yes, that is what I think." You are not required to agree, but your own opinion will be better informed.
