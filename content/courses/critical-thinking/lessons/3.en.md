---
title: Practice
questions:
  - question: A post in your volunteering network says that a new law will close all youth centres in your region next month. It has no link and many angry comments. What is the best first step?
    options:
      - text: Share it quickly, so people can react in time.
      - text: Look for the official text or a trusted news report, and check who wrote the post.
        correct: true
      - text: Ignore it, because most posts online are false.
      - text: Believe it, because so many people are commenting.
    explanation: Checking the source and finding the original text gives you facts you can act on. Sharing without checking spreads possible errors, and dismissing everything is not analysis either. Many comments show interest, not truth.
  - question: Your team wants to hold the summer festival on Saturday, as always. Attendance was low last year. Which question shows critical thinking?
    options:
      - text: Why change something that has always worked?
      - text: Who is going to complain if we move it?
      - text: What can we learn from last year's low attendance, and what do participants say about Saturday?
        correct: true
      - text: Can we find someone to blame for last year?
    explanation: The strongest question uses evidence from last year and invites other views. It goes after causes rather than habit or blame, so the team can test other options.
  - question: You read an article that supports exactly what you already believed about volunteering. What should you do?
    options:
      - text: Check its evidence with the same care you would use for an article you disagree with.
        correct: true
      - text: Share it, since it confirms what you think.
      - text: Distrust it, because it agrees with you.
    explanation: We are all more likely to accept information that fits our views. Applying the same standards to both kinds of articles keeps your thinking independent. Rejecting something only because it agrees with you is as unreasoned as accepting it.
  - question: Two volunteers disagree about whether to hand out leaflets or use social media to reach older residents. Which approach reflects thinking from different perspectives?
    options:
      - text: Vote straight away and move on.
      - text: Choose the option of the person with more experience.
      - text: Do both without checking, to avoid argument.
      - text: Ask older residents how they get their news, then compare the options.
        correct: true
    explanation: Asking the people you want to reach gives you a view you did not have. Voting or deferring to experience can still miss what the audience actually does, and doing both without thinking wastes effort.
---

Read these situations and think about what questions you would ask.

Your coordinator says that "everyone knows" that volunteers who are paid expenses leave faster. You have not seen any evidence.

A video claims that a simple diet can prevent illness, and it is shared by a friend you trust.

At work, two colleagues give you opposite advice about how to answer an angry customer.
