---
title: Forty students and a weather alert
---

Mateus is on the team that organises a beach clean-up near Cascais. At 8:15, forty fifteen-year-olds are due off a coach at nine, and the forecast changes: rain and strong wind from noon. The coordinator, Inês, is stuck in traffic and her phone goes to voicemail. Everyone looks at Mateus, because he has the group list and the keys to the equipment shed.

He sees three options. He can cancel, run the clean-up as planned, or shorten it to two hours on the sheltered side of the beach. He writes down what he knows: the students' age, a café nearby that could take the group in, the coach coming back at 13:00. He cannot know whether the forecast is right, and waiting for a clearer one would mean deciding at nine, with the coach already at the gate.

He chooses the short version, to finish by 11:30. He texts Inês the plan and his reasons, and tells the teachers that the group will go to the café if the wind picks up before then.

The rain starts at 11:40, when the last bags are already by the road. Inês writes back that she would have chosen the same, and that next time they should ask the school for a teacher's phone number a day ahead.

Think about your own experience:

- Think of a decision you had to make quickly, with little information. What did you base it on?
- When have you put off a choice because you wanted to be sure? What did waiting cost?
- Think of a decision of yours that turned out badly. What did you do next?
