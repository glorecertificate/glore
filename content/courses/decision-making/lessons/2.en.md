---
title: What decision making means
---

This is the GloRe definition of the skill:

> Ability to weigh the available options, choose one in good time, and take responsibility for the outcome.

Decision making is the part that comes after you know what the possible answers are. Problem solving finds options. Here the question is which one you pick, when, and what you do with the result.

## Weighing the available options

Start by saying what the options are, including the one where you do nothing. Then ask what matters most in this case: cost, time, safety, fairness to the people involved. Comparing each option against those points is more reliable than going with the one that feels best in the moment.

A youth centre has money for one thing this term: a new sound system or a weekly cooking class. The volunteers list who would use each, how often, and what it would cost to keep running. The cooking class wins because forty young people would use it every week, while the sound system would be used a few times a year.

## Choosing in good time

Information is never complete. Some decisions need an hour of checking and others need a minute. A useful question is "What would I need to know to change my mind?" If you cannot answer it, more research will not help. If you can, find that one fact and stop there.

A student has to pick a thesis topic by Friday. Two topics look equally good. Because the risk of either is small, she picks the one with a supervisor she already knows, and schedules a check-in in a month in case it is not working.

## Taking responsibility for the outcome

Once you choose, the result belongs to you, good or bad. That means telling the people affected what you decided and why, adjusting if new facts appear, and looking back afterwards. A decision that went wrong is still useful if you learn which assumption was weak.

At work, a new team member in a café has to decide whether to refund a customer whose order was late. She has no manager at hand. She refunds half, notes the reason, and tells her manager at the end of the shift. The manager does not agree on the amount, but values that she decided and reported it.

## Deciding with others

Some decisions affect a group. Asking for a few opinions before you decide helps you see what you missed, and it makes people more likely to support the result. It does not mean everyone must agree. After listening, someone still has to choose, and if that is you, say so.
