title: Practice
questions:
  - question: Two volunteers disagree about which of two venues to book for a youth festival, and the booking closes tonight. What helps most?
    options:
      - text: Book the venue suggested by the person who argues most strongly.
      - text: Write down what matters (cost, access, capacity), compare both venues against it, and book before the deadline.
        correct: true
      - text: Wait one more day in the hope that a third option appears.
    explanation: A short list of criteria turns an argument about preferences into a comparison, and the deadline keeps the decision from drifting. Choosing the loudest voice ignores the facts, and waiting gives up the choice you have for one you might not get.
  - question: You made a scheduling choice for the team and it caused a clash with a school trip. What is the best next step?
    options:
      - text: Blame the missing information so nobody thinks it was your decision.
      - text: Change the schedule quietly and hope no one notices.
      - text: Wait for someone else to bring it up.
      - text: Say what you decided and why, fix what can be fixed, and note what you will check next time.
        correct: true
    explanation: Responsibility means owning the choice even when the result is poor. Explaining, repairing, and learning keeps trust in your next decisions. Blaming or hiding teaches the team nothing and makes people trust you less.
  - question: You must choose a thesis topic by Friday. You still do not know which of two good topics is easier to research. What do you do?
    options:
      - text: Choose with what you know now and plan a review in a few weeks.
        correct: true
      - text: Keep reading until you are certain.
      - text: Ask five friends and go with the majority.
      - text: Let the supervisor pick without telling her your preferences.
    explanation: When options are close and you cannot be sure, the cost of a small mistake is lower than the cost of delay. A planned review lets you correct course. Waiting for certainty rarely arrives, and handing the choice to others removes your part in it.
  - question: A decision about the rota will change the weekends of eight volunteers. How should you proceed?
    options:
      - text: Decide alone, because group opinions only slow things down.
      - text: Ask everyone to vote and follow the result, even if it clearly breaks a safety rule.
      - text: Ask the volunteers affected for a short input, then decide and explain how you weighed it.
        correct: true
      - text: Send the new rota without comment and answer complaints later.
    explanation: Asking briefly brings in facts you may not have, such as exams or family duties, and shows respect. You still have to decide, and explaining your reasons helps people accept it. Voting fails when some options cannot be allowed, and deciding in silence causes avoidable friction.
  - question: At a workplace you have two similar internship offers and have been comparing them for a week. What is a sensible step?
    options:
      - text: Compare them for another week to be absolutely sure.
      - text: Notice that the options are close, so either is likely to work out, and choose by one tie-breaker such as travel time.
        correct: true
      - text: Accept both and cancel the one you like less at the last minute.
      - text: Pick whichever answers your message first without comparing anything.
    explanation: When options are nearly equal, more comparison adds little, so a single tie-breaker is enough. Accepting both harms other people, and picking by reply speed ignores your own priorities.
---

Read these situations and think about how you would decide.

At a summer camp, the activity you planned for the afternoon needs a second adult, and the other volunteer has not arrived. The children are already waiting.

For a class presentation, your group has three possible topics and one hour left in the meeting.

At a part-time job, a customer asks for something that is not in the rules, and your supervisor is on a break.
