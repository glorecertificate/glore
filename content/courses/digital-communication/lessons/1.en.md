---
title: The message that went wrong
---

Joonas is a volunteer in Tartu with a food-sharing project. Forty volunteers collect surplus food from shops and bring it to a community fridge. Joonas looks after the group chat.

On Monday evening he types a quick message: "Pickup changed, be there at 7." He sends it and goes to bed. On Tuesday morning, six people have turned up at 7 a.m. at the wrong shop, two have turned up at 7 p.m. at the right one, and a few have asked in the chat, "Changed to what?" Someone has replied with a thumbs-up and nobody knows what it means.

Joonas feels embarrassed. He writes again, slowly this time: "Hi everyone. The Wednesday pickup at the Kesk shop moves from 18:00 to 19:00. Please react with a tick if you can come." He also pins the message, adds the change to the shared calendar and, for the two volunteers who never open the chat, sends a short email.

By evening, thirty-two ticks have appeared, and the volunteers who cannot make it have said so.

Reflect on your own digital habits:

- When did a message of yours, or one you received, get misunderstood? What was missing?
- Which tools do you use most to talk with others, and which one do you avoid? Why?
- What was the last new app or platform you learned, and how did you learn it?
