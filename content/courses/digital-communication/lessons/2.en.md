---
title: What digital communication means
---

This is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to effectively and efficiently communicate through digital media, being familiar with new ICT tools.

The definition has three parts: effective, efficient and familiar with tools.

## Communicating effectively

Effective means the other person understands what you meant. On a screen, you have no face, tone or gesture to help, so the words must do the work. A good digital message says who it is for, what you need or what changed, and by when. Read it once before sending and ask yourself whether someone who joined five minutes ago would understand it.

Tone is easy to lose. "Fine." can sound angry or relaxed. A greeting, a "thanks" or an emoji in the right place makes a message friendlier. In sensitive conversations, such as a disagreement or bad news, a call is often better than text.

At a youth centre, you announce a workshop in the group chat. Instead of "workshop tomorrow", you write: "Photo workshop, Friday 16:00, room 2. Bring a phone. Reply 'yes' if you are coming."

## Communicating efficiently

Efficient means using the right tool and not wasting other people's time. A quick question fits a chat. A detailed request or a document fits an email. A discussion with many opinions fits a call. A shared file is better than five versions sent by email.

Efficiency also means respect for attention. Keep subject lines clear, avoid replying to everyone when it is not needed, and agree on hours when people are expected to reply. In a study group, you agree to use one chat for the project and one for jokes, so that important messages are not lost.

## Being familiar with new ICT tools

ICT tools change quickly, and nobody knows them all. Being familiar means you are willing to try a new tool, find the basic functions, look for a tutorial and ask when you are stuck. It also means knowing the basics of safety: strong passwords, checking who you are sharing a file with, and not publishing other people's photos or personal details without their permission.

At work, your team moves from email to a project board. You spend twenty minutes in the first week learning how to create a task and tag a colleague. After that, you help another volunteer who finds it difficult.

Online meetings are a good example of all three parts: you test your microphone, mute when you are not speaking, use the chat for links and send a short summary afterwards.
