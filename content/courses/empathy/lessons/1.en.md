---
title: The quiet boy at the homework club
---

Katarina volunteers at a homework club for newly arrived children in Ljubljana. On Tuesdays she works with Deniz, who is eleven and has been in the country for two months. For three weeks he has kept his eyes on the table and answered every question with "fine."

Today his worksheet is still blank, and he keeps pulling at the sleeve of his jumper. Katarina's first thought is to tell him, kindly, that he needs to finish it. Instead she tries to picture the afternoon from his side: a new school, a language he is still learning, a room where everyone else seems to know the rules.

She puts her pencil down and says, "This looks like a lot of new words. Do you want to do the first one together, or should we just talk for a minute?" Deniz looks up for the first time. He says quietly that he misses his friends. Katarina does not try to fix it. She says that sounds hard, and asks what his friends were like.

They do not finish the worksheet that day. At the end Deniz asks if he can come a little early next week.

Think about your own experience:

- Think of a time someone understood how you felt without you having to explain. What did they do or say?
- When did you misread what another person was going through? What did you learn later?
- Who is hardest for you to put yourself in the place of, and why?
