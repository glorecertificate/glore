---
title: What empathy means
---

This is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to understand or feel what another person is experiencing by putting oneself in their place.

Empathy starts with attention. You notice that someone is quiet, tense or excited, and you wonder what is behind it. Then you use what you know about them, and about being human, to imagine how the situation looks from where they stand.

## Understanding what another person is experiencing

This is the thinking side of empathy. You pick up on words, tone, silence and body language, and you build a picture of what the person is dealing with. You can do this even when you have never been in the same situation.

At a youth centre, a girl keeps arriving late to the cooking workshop. You could decide she does not care. If you stop to think, you may realise she looks after her younger brothers until her mother comes home.

## Feeling what another person feels

This is the feeling side. When a friend tells you that her grandmother is in hospital, you may feel a heavy weight in your own chest. That reaction is useful, because it tells you the news matters, and it makes your reply warmer.

You do not need to feel exactly the same, and you do not need to fix the problem. Often it is enough to say, "That sounds really hard. I'm here." Be careful with the other direction too: if you take on every feeling around you, you get tired and you help less. Empathy works best when you stay close to the person and still keep your own footing.

## Putting yourself in their place

This means setting aside your own point of view for a moment and asking, "If I were in their situation, with their history and their worries, what would this be like?" It also means checking, because you can be wrong. "Am I understanding this right?" is a good question, and the answer corrects the picture.

In a group project at university, a teammate who always sends his part at the last minute says he is struggling. Instead of getting annoyed, you ask what is making it hard. He explains that he works nights. Together you move the deadline for his part and agree on a mid-week check-in.

At work, a new colleague from another country is silent in team lunches. Think about being the newest person, in a second language, with jokes you do not know yet. Inviting her into the conversation with an easy question, and leaving her free to say little, puts empathy to work.

## Empathy and agreement

You can understand why someone feels angry without agreeing that they are right, and you can understand a person's choice without making it yours. What you decide to do is still up to you.
