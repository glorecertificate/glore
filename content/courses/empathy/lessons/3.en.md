title: Practice
questions:
  - question: At a food bank, an older man refuses a bag and says sharply that he does not need charity. Which reply shows empathy?
    options:
      - text: Everyone needs help sometimes. Please just take it.
      - text: It can be hard to accept help. Would you like to sit for a minute? There is no pressure.
        correct: true
      - text: Fine, then. Next person, please.
    explanation: The second reply recognises the feeling behind the words (pride, maybe embarrassment) and gives him room. The first dismisses the feeling and pushes, and the third ends the contact and leaves him alone with it.
  - question: A fellow volunteer, João, has been late three times and snaps at people. What is the most empathic first step?
    options:
      - text: Tell the coordinator that he is unreliable.
      - text: Snap back, so he knows how it feels.
      - text: Ignore it, because his mood is not your business.
      - text: Ask him privately how he is doing, and listen before offering advice.
        correct: true
    explanation: Behaviour often has a cause you cannot see. Asking in private shows you noticed him as a person, not only the lateness, and listening first lets you learn what is going on. The other options judge him or leave him alone with whatever is happening.
  - question: A classmate is crying because she failed an exam she studied hard for. Which reply helps her most?
    options:
      - text: You studied so much. That must be really disappointing.
        correct: true
      - text: It is only one exam. It does not matter.
      - text: I failed one too, and mine was much worse. Let me tell you.
    explanation: The first reply names what she is feeling and why, so she feels understood. The second shrinks the problem, and the third moves the attention to you. Sharing a similar story can help sometimes, but not before the other person feels heard.
  - question: You are told that a teenager in your sports club often stays silent when the group chooses an activity. Which approach uses empathy and checks it?
    options:
      - text: Decide that he is shy and choose for him.
      - text: Assume he feels the way you did at his age.
      - text: Imagine how it feels to be new in a group, then ask him privately what he would like and whether the way you decide suits him.
        correct: true
      - text: Say nothing, because the quiet ones are always fine.
    explanation: Imagining his place gives you a starting point, and asking tests it. Guessing and deciding for him, or assuming he feels what you felt, replaces his experience with yours. Saying nothing misses the chance to include him.
