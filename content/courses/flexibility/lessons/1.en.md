---
title: Rain at ten o'clock
---

Ilaria volunteers at a public library in Bologna. Every first Saturday of the month, the team runs a storytelling morning for children in the library garden. At nine, the sky is clear, eight children are signed up, and the plan is ready: three stories under the big tree, then a drawing game.

At ten, the clouds roll in. By ten past, it is raining hard. Two volunteers text that they are ill. At the same time, a group of fifteen children arrives from a nearby school, because their teacher heard about the event and wanted to join.

Ilaria feels her pulse rise. For a moment she wants to cancel. Then she makes a quick list of what she has: a reading room that fits twenty, two other volunteers, and a stack of picture books. She asks Beatriz to greet the school group and move them inside. She asks Karim to set up the cushions, and she herself shortens the stories so that more children can listen.

The drawing game turns into a collective picture on the wall, because there are not enough tables. The children seem not to mind. When the parents pick them up, several ask when the next Saturday is.

Think about your own experience:

- When did a plan fall apart at the last minute? What was your first reaction, and what did you do next?
- What helps you stay calm when things change suddenly?
- Is there a change you are resisting at the moment? What might happen if you tried a different approach?
