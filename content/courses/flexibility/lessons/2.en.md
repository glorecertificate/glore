---
title: What flexibility means
---

This is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to adjust to new environments and change, and to adapt to unexpected or challenging situations. Ability to understand different contexts and to act accordingly to cope with new situations and environments.

Flexibility is staying useful when things do not go as planned: you keep your goals and change how you reach them.

## Adjusting to new environments and change

A new place comes with new routines, people and unwritten rules. At first, you are a beginner again, and that can be uncomfortable. Flexibility is accepting this feeling as part of the process, instead of fighting it or hiding it.

Change shows up in small ways too: a meeting moved by an hour, a task handed to someone else, a new tool replacing the old one. Your first reaction may be irritation. Notice it, take a breath, and then ask what you can do with the new situation.

A volunteer on a six-month Erasmus+ project arrives in a small town in Slovakia and finds that her flat has no internet and the supermarket closes at noon on Saturdays. She changes her routine, learns when the shop opens, and uses the library for her online tasks. After a few weeks, the new rhythm feels normal.

## Adapting to unexpected or challenging situations

Unexpected things happen in every project: bad weather, a missing volunteer, a delivery that does not arrive, a person who needs more help than you planned for. A flexible person first checks what has changed and what is still possible. Then they pick a new approach, try it, and change it again if needed.

Under pressure, it helps to separate the goal from the plan. The goal is that children enjoy a morning of stories. The plan, under the tree, was one way of reaching it. If the plan breaks, you look for another way of reaching the same goal. Keeping the goal clear stops you from drifting into a different activity because it is easier.

During a university group project, a teammate drops out two weeks before the deadline. The group splits his section, drops a chart that is not essential, and moves the practice presentation earlier. They hand in a smaller project that is finished.

## Understanding different contexts and acting accordingly

Different settings call for different behaviour. How you talk to a ten-year-old at a youth club is different from how you talk to a municipal officer. A casual start-up office has different habits from a formal public agency. Flexibility is noticing these differences and adjusting your style, your words and your pace, without becoming someone else.

To read a new context, observe before you act. Watch how people greet each other, how decisions are made, who speaks and who listens. Ask a colleague what is expected. Then adjust, and check whether your adjustment works.

On her first day at a care home, a volunteer notices that residents prefer a slow, quiet start to the day. She puts away her energetic games, sits down for a chat with a cup of tea, and starts the music session later.

## Flexibility and your limits

If a change is unsafe, unfair or goes against your values, you say so. Flexibility includes knowing when to adapt and when to ask for something different.
