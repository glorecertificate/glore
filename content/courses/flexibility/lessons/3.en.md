title: Practice
questions:
  - question: You are leading a park clean-up when a heavy rain starts. Half the group wants to go home and half wants to stay. What is the most flexible response?
    options:
      - text: Cancel the activity immediately, since the plan has failed.
      - text: Tell everyone to keep working in the rain because the plan is the plan.
      - text: Ask what people are comfortable with, move to a covered task such as sorting what is already collected, and reschedule the rest.
        correct: true
    explanation: Flexibility keeps the goal (a cleaner park, a useful morning) while changing the way you reach it. Cancelling drops the goal at the first obstacle, and insisting on the original plan puts people's comfort and safety second to the schedule.
  - question: At your first day in a new job, you notice that colleagues email each other even for short questions, while you are used to asking in person. What do you do?
    options:
      - text: Observe for a few days, ask a colleague how the team prefers to communicate, and adapt while keeping some face-to-face contact where it helps.
        correct: true
      - text: Keep asking in person, because it is faster.
      - text: Switch entirely to email, even for colleagues at the next desk.
    explanation: Reading the context before acting shows respect for how the team already works, and checking with a colleague avoids guessing. Staying with your habit ignores the norm, and giving up all personal contact loses something that may be useful.
  - question: A youth workshop you organise has to move to another room at short notice, and the new room has no projector. The presentation was your main tool. What do you do?
    options:
      - text: Postpone the workshop until the projector is back.
      - text: Read the slides aloud from your laptop.
      - text: Insist on moving the workshop back to the old room, even if it is booked.
      - text: Turn the presentation into a discussion using flip charts and printed handouts, and keep the learning goal the same.
        correct: true
    explanation: The goal is that participants learn something, and the projector was only one tool. Switching to a discussion adapts the method to the new space. Postponing loses the session, reading slides aloud is dull and dependent on your laptop, and forcing a room swap ignores that someone else booked it.
  - question: A coordinator announces that your team will switch to a new scheduling app next week. You liked the old one. What is the most helpful attitude?
    options:
      - text: Refuse to use the new app until the coordinator proves it is better.
      - text: Try it for a couple of weeks, write down what works and what does not, and share specific feedback.
        correct: true
      - text: Use it silently and complain to the others.
      - text: Keep using the old app and tell the coordinator later.
    explanation: Giving it a real try and noting concrete points allows an honest assessment and helps the team improve the change. Refusing or ignoring it makes you the obstacle, and complaining without feedback spreads frustration without changing anything.
  - question: 'Two members of your team have a very different style of working: one plans everything in advance, the other prefers to improvise. How do you adjust to both?'
    options:
      - text: Ask each how they like to work, agree on a short plan with room for adjustments, and adapt how you communicate with each of them.
        correct: true
      - text: Side with whoever you agree with most.
      - text: Tell them to follow one style so the team is consistent.
      - text: Avoid working with either of them.
    explanation: Different contexts and people call for different approaches. Asking and agreeing on a plan that leaves room for both styles uses the strengths of each. Taking sides, forcing one style or avoiding them solves nothing and may create tension.

Read these situations and think about how you would respond.

At a refugee support centre, the language teacher is absent and you are asked to run the class with no preparation.

On the day of a charity run, the route is blocked by roadworks, and the start is in forty minutes.

In an Erasmus+ project abroad, the host organisation changes your tasks completely in your second week.
