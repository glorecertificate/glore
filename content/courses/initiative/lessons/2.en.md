---
title: What initiative means
---

This is the skill definition we use in GloRe:

> Ability to notice what needs doing and to start acting on it without waiting to be asked.

Initiative starts before the task exists. Autonomy is about doing a given task without being supervised. Initiative is about seeing that a task is missing and picking it up.

## Noticing what needs doing

Initiative begins with attention to what is around you: a job nobody does, a repeated question, a step that wastes time. Notice what annoys other people, because irritation often points at a gap you could fill.

At a university, Dilara sees that her group's notes are scattered across five chat threads and that people keep asking where the last version is. Nobody has been given the job of collecting them.

## Starting without waiting to be asked

This is the visible part. You do the first small step, such as a draft, a sign or a message, instead of waiting for permission to start. A small first step is easier to take and easier for others to react to than a big plan.

Dilara creates a shared folder, moves the latest notes into it and posts the link. It takes ten minutes. Two days later, everybody uses it.

At work, a trainee in a bookshop notices that customers ask about the same ten things every day. She writes a one-page answer sheet and shows it to her manager, who prints it and puts it at the till.

## Knowing when to check in

Starting does not mean acting alone on everything. If your idea affects other people, uses money, or involves children or sensitive information, tell the person responsible before you begin, or after the first test if it is harmless. Elias in the story could safely try the table first. He would not have been free to invite children from outside the centre without asking.

## Following through

A good idea needs more than a first step. Keep checking whether it works, ask for feedback and adjust. If it does not work, learn from it, make it smaller and try again.

At a local association, a volunteer proposes a monthly clean-up in a park. Only two people come the first time. She asks neighbours what stopped them, learns that the time clashes with a market, and moves the date. Eleven people come the next month.
