title: Practice
questions:
  - question: As a library volunteer, you notice that the children's shelf is in no order and kids struggle to find books. Nobody has asked you to deal with it. What shows initiative?
    options:
      - text: Wait until the manager assigns the task.
      - text: Reorganise the whole library over the weekend without telling anyone.
      - text: Tell the manager what you noticed, offer to sort that shelf this week, and start once she agrees.
        correct: true
    explanation: You noticed a need and offered to act, so the idea is yours. Telling the manager first matters because moving a whole library affects staff and visitors. Waiting wastes the chance, and a silent weekend project can surprise everyone.
  - question: Newcomers to your volunteering group are confused because the event details in the team chat are out of date. What is the best first step?
    options:
      - text: Post the correct details, ask the group to confirm them, and pin the message.
        correct: true
      - text: Complain in the chat that nobody ever updates it.
      - text: Assume someone else will fix it soon.
      - text: Wait for the coordinator to notice the problem.
    explanation: The problem is clear and the fix is small and harmless, so acting now saves everybody time. Asking the group to confirm catches mistakes. Complaining or waiting leaves the confusion in place.
  - question: You want to sell homemade snacks at your school to raise money for the project. What is the right way to take this initiative?
    options:
      - text: Start collecting money tomorrow and explain later.
      - text: Drop the idea because nobody asked for it.
      - text: Ask the coordinator to organise it for you.
      - text: Write a short plan with what you need and who is affected, propose it to the coordinator, and start the parts you are allowed to do.
        correct: true
    explanation: A short plan makes your idea easy to approve, and money is a case where you should check first. Starting immediately can break rules, and dropping the idea or handing it over means it never happens.
  - question: You started a weekly language cafe and only two people came. What is the most useful reaction?
    options:
      - text: Conclude that nobody is interested and stop.
      - text: Ask people why they did not come, change one thing such as the time or place, and try again.
        correct: true
      - text: Blame the others for not promoting it.
      - text: Keep everything exactly the same and hope more people come.
    explanation: A low turnout tells you something that can be tested. Asking, changing one thing and trying again is how initiative improves. Quitting after one try wastes the effort, and repeating the same plan does not give a new result.
  - question: At a festival, you see a visitor who looks dizzy and unwell. Which action shows initiative within the right limits?
    options:
      - text: Offer your own medicine from your bag.
      - text: Look for the coordinator and leave the visitor where they are.
      - text: Stay with the person and call the first-aid team or coordinator right away.
        correct: true
    explanation: Initiative here means acting immediately and staying with the person. It also means knowing your limits. Health situations belong to trained people, so you call them. Giving medicine is risky, and walking away leaves the person alone.

Read these situations and think about what you would do.

You are a new volunteer at an animal shelter. You see that the visitor sign-in sheet is always messy and several visitors leave without being greeted.

Your study group has a deadline in a week, and no one has made a plan.

At your part-time job, a regular customer asks for a product you do not stock, and you have heard the same request before.
