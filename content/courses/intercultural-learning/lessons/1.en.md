---
title: 'Seeing your own lens'
---

Luca is a volunteer in a community kitchen in Porto. Samira, a volunteer from Morocco, arrives twenty minutes after the agreed start every day. Luca grows irritated. In his experience, arriving late means you do not care.

A week later he asks her, curious and without blame, how people in her home town think about time. Samira explains that in her family, meeting people and greeting them properly matters more than the clock, and that she did not know the kitchen counted minutes so strictly. They agree on a start time and on a way to greet the team once the work is done.

## What happened

Luca's first reaction came from his own lens: the habits and values he grew up with. Nobody is without one. Intercultural learning starts when you notice the lens, and it grows when you ask before you judge.

Culture is not a label that explains a person. Two people from the same country can differ a lot, and one person can belong to several cultures at once.

## Think about it

- Describe a time something unfamiliar irritated you. What did you assume?
- What could you have asked instead?
