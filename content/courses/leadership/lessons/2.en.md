---
title: What leadership means
---

This is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to motivate, activate, and involve group members towards a common goal.

A leader does not need a title or the loudest voice. Leadership happens whenever someone helps a group move together toward something it wants to achieve. It can be the captain of a sports team, the person who organises a study group, or a volunteer who steps in when the plan is unclear.

## A common goal

Groups work better when everyone knows what they are aiming for and why it matters. A good goal is clear, shared and specific enough that people can tell when they have reached it. "Make the common room welcoming by tonight" works better than "do some painting."

At a summer camp, the team leader says: "By Friday, each child should have taken part in at least one activity they chose themselves." The volunteers can now plan their days around that.

## Motivating

To motivate is to help people want to contribute. Attention does most of the work here. Explain why the task matters. Thank people for what they actually did. Notice effort, not only results. Be honest when things are hard, and show that you are willing to do the same work as everyone else.

People are motivated by different things: some by learning, some by company, some by seeing the impact. If a student on your team looks bored, you can ask what she would like to try, instead of assuming she doesn't care.

## Activating

To activate is to turn motivation into action. This means that everyone knows what they should do, by when, and with what support. Break the goal into tasks, and match tasks to people's strengths and interests. Check in early, so small problems do not grow.

In a university group project, you might say: "Let's decide who does research, who writes and who designs the slides. Can we meet on Wednesday to see where we are?" The group leaves with a plan.

## Involving

To involve is to make sure each person has a voice and a part. Ask for ideas before giving yours. Invite quieter members directly and with kindness, and make room for different ways of contributing. When people help shape a plan, they feel it belongs to them.

At work, a new team member has not spoken in two meetings. You ask her privately if she wants to prepare one part of the next presentation. She says yes, and her section is the one that gets the best questions.

## Sharing the lead

A leader who decides everything leaves the others waiting for instructions. A leader who helps the group decide, share work and learn leaves the group stronger. Sometimes the best thing a leader does is step back and let someone else lead.
