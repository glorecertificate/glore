---
title: Practice
questions:
  - question: You are leading a team of volunteers for a beach clean-up. People arrive and stand around. What should you do first?
    options:
      - text: Start working alone, so the others follow.
      - text: Give out roles quickly and without explanation.
      - text: Welcome everyone, explain what you want to achieve today and why it matters, then ask who would like to do what.
        correct: true
      - text: Wait until someone asks you what to do.
    explanation: A shared goal gives the work a direction, and asking people to choose tasks involves them from the start. Handing out roles with no reason, or waiting, leaves people unsure of why they are there.
  - question: Halfway through a long event, the volunteers look tired and discouraged. Which action best motivates them?
    options:
      - text: Remind them that they chose to be here.
      - text: Thank them for what they have done so far, show what has been achieved, and offer a short break before the last part.
        correct: true
      - text: Say the work is easy and they should not complain.
    explanation: Recognition of real effort, a view of progress and a short rest can restore energy. Reminding people about their choice or dismissing their tiredness makes them feel unseen.
  - question: In a group meeting, one volunteer, Leila, has not said anything. How can a leader involve her?
    options:
      - text: Ask her in front of everyone to give her opinion immediately.
      - text: Assume she has nothing to add.
      - text: Move on, because people speak when they are ready.
      - text: Invite her kindly, for example by asking if she would like to take on a part of the task, or by talking after the meeting.
        correct: true
    explanation: A warm and low-pressure invitation gives her a way to contribute in a manner that suits her. Putting her on the spot can make her more withdrawn, and ignoring her keeps her out.
  - question: Your group has a deadline in three days and nobody knows who is doing what. Which move shows leadership?
    options:
      - text: Break the work into tasks, ask who wants which one, and set a short check-in for tomorrow.
        correct: true
      - text: Do all the work yourself so that it is done right.
      - text: Send a message saying "We need to work harder."
    explanation: Clear tasks, a say in who does what and an early check-in turn a shared deadline into a plan. Doing everything yourself leaves the group out, and a general message gives nobody anything to act on.
---

Read these situations and think about how you would lead.

At a refugee support centre, the weekly welcome lunch has no volunteers signed up for the next month.

Your sports club wants to raise money for new equipment, but people have different ideas about how.

In a group project, one teammate dominates the discussion and two others never speak.
