---
title: Learning the radio desk
---

Sofia is a volunteer at a community radio station in Thessaloniki. Her coordinator has asked her to edit the weekly youth show, which means learning audio software she has never opened. The first evening she watches three long tutorial videos and remembers almost nothing the next day.

She feels stuck until she thinks back to how she learned to cook: she never read a recipe through, she just made a mess with someone beside her. So she asks Giorgos, who edits the news, if she can sit next to him for an hour and try the buttons herself while he explains.

That hour does more than the videos. Afterwards she writes down what she wants: to cut and clean one interview by the end of the month, and to add music by the end of the next. She puts a twenty-minute practice slot in her diary on Tuesdays and Fridays, and asks Giorgos to listen to her first edit.

Two weeks later, her first cut sounds rough, and she notices that she keeps skipping the practice slot on Fridays, when she is tired. She moves it to Wednesday evening, and it starts to stick.

Think about your own experience:

- Think of something you learned well. What helped: reading, watching, practising, talking, or something else?
- Think of something you tried to learn and gave up on. What would you do differently now?
- What would you like to learn in the next three months, and why does it matter to you?
