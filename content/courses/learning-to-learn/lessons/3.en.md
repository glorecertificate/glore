title: Practice
questions:
  - question: You volunteer at a library and want to learn to run storytelling sessions for children. You have read two books and still feel unprepared. What helps most?
    options:
      - text: Read more books on the subject until you feel ready.
      - text: Wait for a training course to be offered.
      - text: Ask to assist at a session, run a few minutes yourself, and ask the librarian for feedback afterwards.
        correct: true
    explanation: Learning by doing with feedback suits a practical skill like storytelling, and a small first step is easy to start. More reading builds knowledge but not the skill, and waiting for a course postpones learning with no guarantee it will come.
  - question: A friend says that watching videos is the only real way to learn. You notice that you remember much more after you explain things aloud to someone. What should you do?
    options:
      - text: Use study methods that include explaining aloud, such as teaching a friend or recording yourself, and keep videos as one source.
        correct: true
      - text: Follow your friend's advice, because most people learn from videos.
      - text: Stop using videos altogether.
    explanation: Your own experience is the best evidence of how you learn, and you can use it while still drawing on other methods. Copying someone else's method ignores what works for you, and dropping videos completely throws away a useful resource.
  - question: Which of these is the clearest learning aim for a volunteer in Erasmus+ who is moving to Poland?
    options:
      - text: Learn some Polish.
      - text: Be more fluent in Polish by next year.
      - text: Understand the basics of Polish.
      - text: Be able to order food and ask for directions in Polish within my first six weeks.
        correct: true
    explanation: A good aim says what you will be able to do and by when, so you can plan practice and tell whether you got there. The others are vague, so you could never be sure whether you had succeeded.
  - question: You planned to study for an online course for an hour each evening, but after a week you have done only two sessions. What is the best response?
    options:
      - text: Give up, because you clearly lack the discipline.
      - text: Look at why the evenings did not work, for example tiredness, and move a shorter session to a different time.
        correct: true
      - text: Plan three hours a day for the weekend to catch up.
      - text: Keep the same plan and promise yourself you will try harder.
    explanation: A plan is a first guess. Looking at what got in the way, then adjusting time or length, fixes the cause. Giving up treats a scheduling problem as a character flaw, and long catch-up sessions and promises usually repeat the same pattern.
  - question: A colleague at your summer job is very good at the customer database you need to learn. How do you make the most of this?
    options:
      - text: Watch quietly from a distance so you do not bother them.
      - text: Tell them you will learn it on your own to save their time.
      - text: Ask for fifteen minutes to see how they use it, practise afterwards, and come back with specific questions.
        correct: true
      - text: Ask them to do your part of the work for the first month.
    explanation: A short, specific request respects their time and gets you real examples, and coming back with questions shows you are putting in effort. Watching from afar misses the detail, and refusing help is slower. Asking for the work to be done for you means you do not learn the skill.
