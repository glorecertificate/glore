---
title: A room for the language class
---

Sofia is a volunteer with a small association in Athens that offers free Greek classes to newly arrived families. The group meets in a borrowed flat that is far too small. Twelve adults and eight children are sitting on the floor, and winter is coming.

Sofia knows that the association cannot pay rent. She also remembers that last month she chatted with a woman called Eleni at a neighbourhood fair. Eleni works for a local library, and the stand next to hers belonged to a parish youth group.

That evening Sofia writes a short message to Eleni: "Hi Eleni, we met at the fair. I'm the volunteer from the Greek classes for families. We are looking for a room two mornings a week. I wondered if the library has a space, or if you know who I could ask. We could also help with your children's reading hour in return."

Eleni answers the next day. The library has no room, but the parish group has a hall, and she introduces Sofia by email. A week later the class has a new home, and two volunteers from the parish join the reading hour.

Think about your own network:

- Who do you know outside your usual group, such as people in other associations, schools, shops or offices?
- When did you ask someone for help, or offer it? How did it feel?
- What stops you from starting a conversation with someone you do not know?
