title: Practice
questions:
  - question: At a community festival, you meet the manager of a local sports centre. Your project needs a hall for a weekend. What is the best way to begin the conversation?
    options:
      - text: Ask straight away whether they can lend you the hall for free.
      - text: Introduce yourself and your project in a sentence, ask about their work and what they care about, then mention your idea.
        correct: true
      - text: Hand over a flyer and walk away to meet other people.
    explanation: A connection starts with mutual interest. Asking about their work and listening shows respect and helps you find a link between their goals and yours. Asking at once puts pressure on a stranger, and a flyer alone leaves no relationship.
  - question: You met a journalist at an event and she gave you her card. Two days later, what should you do?
    options:
      - text: Send a short message thanking her, reminding her who you are, and offering a contact for a story on your project.
        correct: true
      - text: Wait until you have a big story, so you don't disturb her.
      - text: Add her to your group email list for every announcement.
    explanation: A quick thank-you while the meeting is fresh keeps the contact alive and offers her something useful. Waiting for months makes you a stranger again, and adding her to every mailing list can feel like spam when she has not agreed to it.
  - question: A local company has helped your association with a donation. Which action best keeps the relationship active?
    options:
      - text: Say thank you at the time and contact them again only when you need more money.
      - text: Send a long email every week about all your activities.
      - text: Thank them publicly, send photos of what the donation made possible, and invite their staff to your next event.
        correct: true
      - text: Mention the company only if they ask.
    explanation: Showing the result of their help and inviting them in turns a one-time gift into a relationship. Contacting them only when you need money makes the link look like a transaction, and a weekly flood of news is more likely to be ignored.
  - question: A volunteer from another association asks if you can help her find speakers for a workshop. You are busy this week. What is the best reply?
    options:
      - text: Ignore the message, since you have nothing to gain from helping.
      - text: Say you are busy this week, offer two names or a time when you can look into it, and ask what she needs.
        correct: true
      - text: Say yes to everything and then forget about it.
    explanation: Contacts last when people are honest and reliable. A clear answer with a real offer shows that you value the relationship, and she will remember it when you need help. Ignoring her closes the door, and a promise you forget damages trust.

Consider these three situations.

You volunteer at an animal shelter and want a nearby vet to offer cheaper check-ups for the animals. You have never met the vet.

You are leaving a youth exchange and have collected the email addresses of eight participants from other countries.

Your sports club has no contact with the local council, and the council has just announced a call for community projects.
