---
title: What open-mindedness and tolerance mean
---

This is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to be open to new ideas, experiences, theories, people and ways of living; ability to adopt a fair and respectful attitude towards opinions, beliefs and practices that differ from one's own.

The definition has two parts. The first is about being open: you are curious about what you do not know yet. The second is about attitude: you treat what differs from you with fairness and respect, even when you do not share it.

## Being open to what is new

Open-mindedness starts with noticing your first reaction. When you meet an idea, a food, a language or a lifestyle that is unfamiliar, you may feel surprise, doubt or discomfort. That is normal. The skill is to pause before deciding that something is wrong or strange, and to ask yourself what you might learn.

At university, a lecturer proposes a theory that contradicts what you learned at school. You do not have to accept it, but you can read it carefully and ask what evidence supports it. You may end up changing your mind or keeping your view with better reasons.

At work, your new team uses a tool and a schedule you have never seen. Trying it for a few weeks before judging tells you more than commenting on the first day.

## A fair and respectful attitude

Fairness means that you give the same attention and respect to people whose views or ways of living differ from yours as to those who agree with you. In practice, this means listening to the whole argument before you answer, asking real questions, and describing the other position in a way that the other person would recognise.

During a debate at a youth centre, a participant argues that the centre should spend its budget on equipment, not trips. You disagree. Instead of interrupting, you ask what experience led to the view. You hear that he has seen trips cancelled twice for lack of money. Now you understand the concern, and you can discuss a solution.

## Questions, not assumptions

Being open means checking your assumptions about groups of people against the person you actually meet. A group is never one person. If you ask a new colleague about her country, she is speaking for herself, not for everyone who comes from it. It is better to ask about her experience than to ask her to explain a whole culture.

## What tolerance does and does not mean

Tolerance means respecting people's right to have different beliefs, customs and ways of life. It does not mean agreeing with everything, and it does not mean accepting behaviour that harms others. Jokes that demean a group, threats, or exclusion are not differences of opinion. If you see them in a project, say that they are not acceptable, and take the matter to the coordinator if it continues.
