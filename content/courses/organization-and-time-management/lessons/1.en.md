---
title: Thirty children and one planting day
---

Ioana coordinates volunteers at a community garden in Cluj-Napoca. This Saturday a primary school class is coming at ten to plant vegetables, and she has four volunteers to help her. On Thursday evening she looks at what is still open: the seedlings have not arrived, there are only twelve pairs of gloves, and nobody has booked the watering cans.

She could start with whatever feels most urgent, but she sits down with a pen first. She writes every task, marks the ones that block the others (no seedlings, no planting), and puts a time next to each. She calls the supplier and gets a delivery for Friday afternoon. She asks the school to bring gloves for the rest. Then she looks at the four volunteers. Dragos has already done the last three set-ups alone, so she gives him the registration table this time and asks Mara to prepare the beds.

On Saturday the seedlings are late by twenty minutes. The group starts with a tour of the garden and a short talk about soil, which Ioana had kept in her plan as a flexible slot. The children plant at half past ten, and everybody leaves on time.

Think about your own experience:

- Think of a time when a deadline was close and you had many things to do. How did you decide what to do first?
- When did you start a task without checking the time, the people or the money you had? What happened?
- Who usually ends up with the most work in your groups, and how does that happen?
