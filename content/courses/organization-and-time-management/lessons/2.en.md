---
title: What organization and time management mean
---

This is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to design tasks by prioritising and planning ahead, in order to achieve one's goals within set deadlines. This includes taking available resources (human, economic, time) into consideration; and sharing activities and responsibilities equitably, considering team resources.

The definition has four parts. Each one answers a different question: what comes first, when does it need to be done, what do we have, and who does what.

## Prioritising and planning ahead

Prioritising means deciding what matters most. A useful test is to ask what has the nearest deadline, and what other tasks are waiting for it. Planning ahead means breaking a goal into steps, putting them in order and giving each a date before you begin.

Before a charity concert at a youth centre, posters must go to the printer first, because the publicity depends on them. Choosing the playlist can wait until the week of the concert. A list with dates next to each task shows this at a glance.

## Achieving goals within deadlines

A plan only helps if it ends on time. Estimate how long each step takes, and add some room for delays, because things are almost always slower than you expect. If a deadline is at risk, say so early, while there is still time to change something.

At university, a final essay due in three weeks becomes five smaller tasks: choose the topic, read, outline, write a draft, revise. Each task gets a date. If the reading takes longer than planned, you see it in week one and not on the night before.

## Taking available resources into consideration

Resources are the people, money and time you actually have. A clean-up day planned for forty people with two volunteers and 200 euros needs a smaller scope: a shorter stretch of riverbank, borrowed tools, a later date. Checking resources first saves you from promising what you cannot deliver.

## Sharing activities and responsibilities equitably

Equitable sharing takes each person's time, skills and energy into account, so the shares can differ. Ask people what they can do instead of guessing, and notice who always gets the heavy or the dull tasks.

At work, a team of six prepares a trade fair stand. One colleague has a long commute, another has a young child, a third is new and wants to learn. The team lead asks everyone about their week, rotates the early shifts and gives the new colleague a task that teaches something. Nobody carries the whole weight, and the stand opens on time.
