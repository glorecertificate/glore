title: Practice
questions:
  - question: The projector at your youth centre breaks twenty minutes before film night for twenty-five people. What is the best first step?
    options:
      - text: Cancel the film night, because the projector is broken.
      - text: Define what you need (show the film to twenty-five people), then list ways to do it.
        correct: true
      - text: Try to repair the projector until it works.
      - text: Wait to see whether someone else has a solution.
    explanation: Describing the goal opens more options than fixing the broken object. You may find a laptop with speakers, a borrowed projector or a bigger screen. Cancelling gives up too early, and fixing or waiting assumes there is only one way.
  - question: Volunteers at your project often arrive late to the Saturday shift. What do you do?
    options:
      - text: Send a stricter reminder to the whole group.
      - text: Replace the volunteers who arrive late.
      - text: Ask them what makes it hard, then try one change, such as a later start, and check the result.
        correct: true
      - text: Accept it, because it always happens.
    explanation: A solution needs the real cause, and the people involved often know it. Testing one change shows whether you found the cause. A stricter message or replacing people treats the symptom, and accepting it gives up.
  - question: Five volunteers must promote a river clean-up with no budget. Which approach is innovative and efficient?
    options:
      - text: Ask the team for ideas, then use what exists, such as local school noticeboards, sports clubs and community group chats.
        correct: true
      - text: Wait until a budget is approved.
      - text: Print posters at your own cost.
      - text: Post once on social media and hope it spreads.
    explanation: Using free channels that already reach the right people is a creative way to work around a lack of money. Asking the team gives you more ideas. Waiting delays the event, paying yourself is not sustainable, and one post alone rarely reaches enough people.
  - question: You can repaint a room with volunteers (100 euros, three weekends) or hire a painter (500 euros, one day). The budget is 150 euros and the room is needed in two months. What do you choose?
    options:
      - text: The painter, because it is faster.
      - text: Neither, because both have drawbacks.
      - text: The painter, and ask for more budget later.
      - text: The volunteers, because it fits the budget and the deadline, and check progress after the first weekend.
        correct: true
    explanation: Comparing options against your limits shows which one works. Here the volunteer option meets both the budget and the deadline. Checking after the first weekend lets you change the plan early if it is slower than expected.
  - question: You tried a new way to sign people in at an event, but the queue got longer. What do you do?
    options:
      - text: Keep the system, because changing it would look like a failure.
      - text: Go back to the old way and never try anything new again.
      - text: Look at what slowed the queue, adjust it or try another option, and note what you learned.
        correct: true
    explanation: A failed attempt tells you something about the problem. Looking at what slowed people down lets you adjust with a better guess next time. Keeping a system that does not work, or giving up on trying new things, wastes what the test taught you.

Read these situations and think about how you would approach the problem.

At a refugee support centre, the language class has twenty people but only twelve chairs and one teacher.

At a festival, the recycling bins are overflowing by noon and the cleaning team is on a break.

At work, two colleagues disagree about which tool to use for a shared report, and the deadline is tomorrow.
