---
title: Five minutes at the school assembly
---

Amira is a volunteer with an environmental association in Cluj. She has spent three months helping to clean a stream near a primary school. Today the head teacher has given her five minutes at the school assembly to invite the children to join the next clean-up.

Amira has written her speech on four pages. In the corridor she can hear a hundred children and she feels her hands go cold. She has spoken at small team meetings, but never in front of this many people, and most of them she has never met.

She decides to cut the pages down to one idea: "Next Saturday we are going to give the stream back to the frogs." She puts the card in her pocket, takes a breath and walks to the front. She looks at a boy in the second row and starts with a question: "Who here has seen a frog in this town?"

Hands go up. She hears her voice settle. She talks slowly, shows a photo of the stream before and after, and finishes by saying the time and the place twice. Afterwards three children ask if their parents can come too.

Take a moment to think:

- When did you have to speak in front of a group, large or small? How did your body feel before and during?
- Which was harder for you, speaking to people you know or to people you do not? Why?
- What helped you the last time you listened to a speaker you enjoyed?
