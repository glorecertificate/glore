---
title: What public speaking means
---

Here is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to communicate clearly and effectively in front of both familiar and unfamiliar people.

It looks short, but it holds three ideas.

## Communicating clearly

Clear means that people can repeat your main point after you finish. A short talk works best around one message, with two or three supporting points. Use simple words, short sentences and one example for each point. If you speak in a second language, simple words are a strength, not a weakness.

At a volunteer training, you explain how to register a new participant. You say, "There are three steps: name, contact, and consent form." People remember three steps. They do not remember a five-minute explanation of the form.

## Communicating effectively

Effective means your talk does what you wanted it to do: informs, invites, convinces or thanks. You get there by thinking about the audience first. What do they already know? What do they care about? What do you want them to do afterwards?

For a university presentation, you ask yourself what the teacher and your classmates want to learn, not what you can fit in. For a job interview, you answer in a short story with a beginning, a challenge and a result.

Your voice and body help. Speak a little more slowly than feels natural, pause between ideas, look at people one at a time and keep your hands relaxed. Slides, photos and objects can help, but they support you and do not replace you.

## Familiar and unfamiliar people

Speaking to friends and colleagues feels safer, but it has its own challenge: they know you and you may care what they think. Speaking to strangers is harder at the start because you do not know how they will react. In both cases, you can prepare in the same way: know your first sentence, know your last sentence and practise the middle out loud.

Nerves are normal. A fast heartbeat is your body getting ready. Slow breathing, arriving early and checking the room all help. If you lose your place, pause, drink some water and go back to your main message.

## Taking questions

Questions are part of speaking in public. Listen to the whole question, repeat it if the room is large and answer briefly. If you do not know the answer, say so and offer to find out.
