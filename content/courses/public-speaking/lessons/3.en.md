---
title: Practice
questions:
  - question: You will present your volunteering project to a local council in five minutes. How should you prepare the content?
    options:
      - text: Choose one main message, add two or three supporting points with a short example for each, and practise out loud.
        correct: true
      - text: Include every detail, so nobody can say that you left something out.
      - text: Memorise a long text word by word and read it quickly.
    explanation: A short talk works when people can remember the main message. Choosing a few points and practising aloud makes you more flexible. Too many details overload the audience, and a memorised text sounds stiff and falls apart if you lose a word.
  - question: Minutes before a talk at a youth exchange, your hands are shaking and your heart is racing. What is the most helpful thing to do?
    options:
      - text: Tell yourself to stop being nervous and hope it passes.
      - text: Cancel your slot and offer the time to someone else.
      - text: Breathe slowly, walk around the room once, and remind yourself of your first sentence.
        correct: true
    explanation: Nerves are your body preparing for action, so trying to switch them off rarely works. Slow breathing and a clear first sentence give your mind something to hold on to. Cancelling avoids the discomfort but also the chance to see that you can do it.
  - question: You are asked to explain your volunteer project to a group of eight-year-olds, and later to an audience of adult sponsors. What should change?
    options:
      - text: Nothing, because the content of the project is the same.
      - text: The examples, vocabulary and length, because each group needs different things.
        correct: true
      - text: Only your volume, so that everybody can hear you.
    explanation: Effective communication starts with the audience. Children need short sentences, concrete images and activity. Sponsors may want results and numbers. The content stays true in both talks, but the way you tell it adapts.
  - question: During a talk at a community event, someone asks a question you cannot answer. What is the best reply?
    options:
      - text: Invent an answer that sounds reasonable so you don't look unprepared.
      - text: Ignore the question and move to the next slide.
      - text: Tell the person the question is not relevant.
      - text: Say that you don't know the answer, and offer to find out and share it afterwards.
        correct: true
    explanation: Honesty builds trust with an audience, and a promise to follow up shows respect. Inventing an answer can be discovered later, and ignoring or dismissing the question makes the room feel unsafe for other people to ask.
---

Consider these three situations.

You volunteer at a library and have to welcome 40 new visitors at the start of a reading night. Some of them are your friends and some you have never met.

You must report your team's progress at an online meeting with a funder. You will have three minutes and the camera will be on.

You are thanking a group of donors at the end of a charity run. The microphone does not work and there is no time to fix it.
