---
title: The missing water bottles
---

Chiara is in charge of supplies at a street festival in Bologna. Two days before the event, she places the order for the volunteers' water: forty crates. On Saturday morning at nine, the delivery arrives, and the driver unloads four crates. Chiara looks at her order form. She typed "4" instead of "40."

Her stomach drops. The festival opens at eleven, it will be thirty degrees, and sixty volunteers are working shifts outside. For a moment she thinks about saying the supplier made a mistake. Nobody has seen the form except her.

She takes a breath and walks to the coordinator, Davide. "I made an error on the order. I wrote four crates instead of forty. I'm sorry. I think we can fix it: there is a supermarket ten minutes away, and I can go now with two volunteers and a car if you agree." Davide is not happy, but he says thanks for telling him straight away, and he approves a small budget.

They are back by ten-thirty. That evening, Chiara adds a second check to the order process and shares it with the team.

Think about your own experience:

- When did you make a mistake in a group? What did you do first?
- What makes it easier or harder for you to say, "That was my responsibility"?
- Think of a person you trust to follow through. What do they do that you could copy?
