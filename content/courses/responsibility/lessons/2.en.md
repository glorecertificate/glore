---
title: What responsibility means
---

This is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to assume one's own actions and to be accountable for them.

Responsibility starts with a simple idea: what you do, or fail to do, has effects, and some of those effects belong to you. Taking responsibility means recognising that and answering for it.

## Assuming your own actions

To assume your actions is to say, "I did that," or "I agreed to that." It applies to the good things too, such as finishing a task or offering to help. It also applies to what you promise: when you say you will bring the projector, other people plan around your word.

It helps to be honest about what is yours and what is not. You are responsible for the part you control: your decisions, your effort, your reaction. You are not responsible for everything that goes wrong around you. If the bus was cancelled and you arrived late, you are not to blame for the cancellation, but you are responsible for letting the team know and for finding another route next time.

At an environmental project, you agree to count the plants in one section of a park. Taking the task on means you either do it or tell the group early that you cannot.

## Being accountable

To be accountable is to be ready to explain what you did, to listen to its effects on others, and to put things right where you can. When something goes wrong, there are three useful steps: say what happened without hiding or exaggerating, say what you will do about it, and ask what the others need from you.

Compare two answers from a student who missed a group presentation rehearsal. One says, "Traffic was terrible, and nobody told me the time." The other says, "I missed it. I misread the message. I'll send you my slides now and take the first slot on Thursday." The second takes responsibility, and the team can work with it.

## Responsibility and learning

Admitting a mistake is uncomfortable, but hiding it is usually worse, because the problem grows and trust decreases. People are more willing to give you important tasks when they know you will tell them early. A mistake that you own and fix often leaves you with more trust than before.

At work, you send a client the wrong file. You tell your supervisor within the hour, send the correct version, and write down a way to avoid it next time.

## Responsibility without guilt

Taking responsibility means being honest and acting. You can feel disappointed and still do what comes next, and you do not need to punish yourself or take the blame for everyone.
