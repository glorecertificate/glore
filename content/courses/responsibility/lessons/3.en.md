---
title: Practice
questions:
  - question: You promised to bring the sound system to a youth-centre party, but you realise on Thursday that you cannot get it. What is the most responsible action?
    options:
      - text: Say nothing and hope someone else brings one.
      - text: Tell the organisers now, explain what happened, and offer to help find an alternative.
        correct: true
      - text: Say the owner cancelled, even though you never confirmed with them.
    explanation: Telling people early gives them time to plan, and offering help shows that you still care about the result. Staying silent leaves them with a surprise, and a made-up excuse damages trust more than the problem itself.
  - question: A group project is late. Your part was finished, but two others were not. During the meeting with the professor, what shows accountability?
    options:
      - text: Point out that your part was ready and the others were late.
      - text: Say sorry for everything, even the parts that were not yours.
      - text: Keep quiet and hope that nobody asks.
      - text: Explain what you finished, offer to help with what remains, and agree on a new date together.
        correct: true
    explanation: Accountability covers your part and the shared result. You can be clear about what is yours without blaming others, and you can offer to help finish the work. Apologising for everything or hiding does not solve the problem.
  - question: You forgot to lock the equipment room after an event, and a laptop was taken. How do you respond?
    options:
      - text: Report it at once, say you forgot to lock the room, and help find out what to do next.
        correct: true
      - text: Say you thought someone else had locked it.
      - text: Say that the room should have had a better lock.
      - text: Quit the volunteering team so you do not have to face it.
    explanation: Being direct about what happened lets the team act quickly, for example by informing the owner. Looking for excuses or leaving avoids responsibility. The lock may be a real issue, but it does not change your part.
  - question: Which sentence shows a person who is taking responsibility for a mistake?
    options:
      - text: It was not really my fault, since the instructions were unclear.
      - text: Mistakes happen, so there is no point talking about it.
      - text: I sent the wrong date. I have emailed everyone the right one and I will double-check dates from now on.
        correct: true
    explanation: This sentence names the mistake, repairs the effect and describes a change. The first shifts the blame, and the second closes the conversation before anything is fixed.
---

Read these situations and decide what taking responsibility would look like.

You are one of three people who agreed to set up chairs for a community meeting. You arrive late and find the other two have done it all.

You told a friend you would help her move on Saturday, then a better offer came up.

At an animal shelter, you notice that you gave a dog the wrong portion of food.
