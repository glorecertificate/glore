---
title: The game night that was too loud
---

Ilse volunteers at a youth centre in Utrecht. She is energetic, full of ideas, and the team trusts her to start things. This month she is organising a game night for forty teenagers.

At the planning meeting she announces the schedule she has already worked out. Two volunteers raise small suggestions. Ilse answers each with a fast "Yes, but" and moves on. During the night, the quieter teenagers drift to the edge of the room, and the helpers look tired.

The next day, Sam, who has been at the centre longer, asks if she has a minute. He says, "You get everyone moving, and that is why people follow you. But last night I felt there was no space for anyone else's ideas." Ilse's face goes warm. Her first reaction is to explain herself. Then she thinks about the "Yes, but" answers, and she recognises the pattern. It happens at university group work too.

That evening she writes two lists on a page: what she is good at, and what she tends to do under pressure. Beside the second list she writes one thing to try at the next meeting: ask for two ideas before presenting hers.

Think about your own experience:

- What do other people usually thank you for, or ask you to help with?
- When did someone's feedback surprise you? What did you do with it?
- Which habit would you like to change, and what is one small step you could try this week?
