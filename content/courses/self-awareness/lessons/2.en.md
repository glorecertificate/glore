---
title: What self-awareness means
---

This is the GloRe definition:

> Ability to identify one's own strengths and weaknesses, learn from them, and improve upon them.

Self-awareness is an honest look at yourself. It works in three steps: you see what you are good at and what you find hard, you learn from what happens, and you work on what you want to change.

## Identifying your strengths and weaknesses

Strengths are the things you do well or enjoy doing, such as staying calm, explaining things, organising a schedule or making people laugh. Weaknesses are the things that cost you effort or often go wrong, such as speaking up, finishing tasks or accepting help.

You find them by paying attention to your own reactions and to what other people say. Notice when time passes quickly, when you feel nervous, and which tasks people bring to you. Ask a friend or a team member, "What do you think I do well? What should I watch out for?" Other people often see patterns you miss.

At an environmental project, you may find that you are quick to set up equipment but you avoid talking to the public.

## Learning from them

A strength or weakness is only information until you look at what it does. After a tense moment or a good day, take five minutes to ask: What happened? What did I do? What would I do again, and what would I change?

Say you panicked when a speaker cancelled at an event. Afterwards you see that you had no backup plan and that you were afraid to ask for help. That is a lesson you can use next time, and it is more helpful than blaming yourself.

Learning also covers your strengths. Knowing that you explain things well can help you decide to train new volunteers, or choose a course.

## Improving upon them

Improvement works best in small, specific steps. "Be more confident" is too big. "Raise my hand once in each meeting" is something you can do and check. You can also use a strength to support a weakness: if you are good at writing, prepare notes for a speech you find stressful.

At work, you may know that you take on too much and then miss deadlines. You decide to say, "Let me check my schedule and answer tomorrow" before agreeing to new tasks. After a month, you look at how it went and adjust.

Look at the good things as clearly as the difficult ones, and be as patient with yourself as you would be with a friend.
