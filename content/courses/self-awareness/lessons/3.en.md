---
title: Practice
questions:
  - question: After a workshop you led, two participants looked bored. Which reflection shows the most self-awareness?
    options:
      - text: They were just tired, so it had nothing to do with me.
      - text: I am a terrible facilitator and should never lead again.
      - text: I spoke for twenty minutes without asking a question. Next time I will add an activity after ten minutes.
        correct: true
    explanation: The third answer describes what you actually did, links it to the result and picks a change you can test. Blaming others skips the learning, and blaming yourself in general terms gives you nothing to improve.
  - question: A team member says you often interrupt others in meetings. You had not noticed. What is the best next step?
    options:
      - text: Tell them they are exaggerating.
      - text: Ask for a recent example, and ask a second person whether they see it too.
        correct: true
      - text: Stay silent in all meetings from now on.
      - text: Wait to see if anyone else mentions it.
    explanation: Asking for an example and checking with another person helps you separate a real pattern from one opinion. Denying the feedback or overcorrecting skips that step, and waiting means missing the chance to learn now.
  - question: You want to improve your confidence when speaking to groups. Which plan is most likely to work?
    options:
      - text: Speak for one minute at each team meeting for a month, and note what went well after each one.
        correct: true
      - text: Try to become a different person by next month.
      - text: Wait until you feel ready.
      - text: Avoid speaking until someone asks you.
    explanation: A small, repeated step that you can measure builds skill and gives you evidence of progress. Goals that are too large, or plans that depend on feeling ready, tend to stall.
  - question: A volunteer coordinator asks what your strengths are. Which answer shows that you know yourself?
    options:
      - text: I am good at everything, so just give me any task.
      - text: I do not really have strengths.
      - text: Everyone says I am great, so I guess that is true.
      - text: I am patient with children and good at planning activities, but I find it hard to speak up in large meetings.
        correct: true
    explanation: A useful answer names specific strengths and a specific difficulty, with examples you could back up. Claiming everything, or nothing, or repeating what others say shows little reflection.
---

Read these situations and think about what you would notice and learn.

You agreed to design the poster for a charity fair. The night before, you realise you have not started, and it is not the first time this has happened.

A friend from your language exchange says you often change the subject when she talks about her problems.

You have just finished your first month of volunteering at an animal shelter, and the coordinator asks how it went.
