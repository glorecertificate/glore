---
title: One dish, many tables
---

Lena volunteers at a neighbourhood centre in Malmö that runs a monthly cooking evening. Newcomers and long-time residents prepare a meal together and eat it at one long table. This month she is in charge of the menu, and she has chosen a stew with pork because it is cheap and everyone likes it.

When the group arrives, she notices that Fatima, who came from Syria a few weeks ago, stands near the door and holds her bag in front of her. Lena goes over and asks if everything is all right. Fatima says quietly that she does not eat pork and did not want to make trouble.

Lena feels her face go hot. She had assumed her menu was neutral. She thanks Fatima for telling her and asks the rest of the group if anyone else has dietary needs. A man mentions he is vegetarian, and a teenager says she is allergic to nuts. Together they change the stew to chicken with a vegetarian version, and Fatima joins the chopping at the counter.

Over dinner, people share what their families eat at celebrations. Lena is surprised by how many ideas for next month come from the table.

Think about your own experience:

- When did you realise that something you thought was "normal" is not normal for everyone?
- Have you ever been the person who felt out of place in a group? What did someone do, or fail to do, that made a difference?
- Which customs or needs in your own life would you want others to know about before they plan something for you?
