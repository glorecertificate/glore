---
title: The information desk at two o'clock
---

Lucía volunteers at the information desk of a music festival outside Valencia. It is two in the afternoon, thirty-five degrees, and a queue of about thirty people has formed. Her phone is ringing, a visitor is asking about the shuttle, and a father arrives out of breath because he cannot find his eight-year-old daughter.

Lucía notices her heart beating fast and her hands getting cold. She also notices a thought: "I cannot handle all of this." She breathes out slowly, twice, and says to the father, "Stay with me. I am going to call our lost-children team now, and we will do it step by step." She follows the protocol from the briefing: description, last place seen, radio call. She asks Hakan, who is next to her, to take the queue and tell people there is a short wait.

Ten minutes later the girl is found at the first-aid tent, with a juice in her hand. The father thanks the team and goes. Lucía's legs feel shaky. She tells Hakan she needs five minutes, drinks some water in the shade and walks around the back of the tent. When the shift ends she mentions the moment to her coordinator, who says it was a good call to take a break.

Think about your own experience:

- Think of a recent moment when you felt under pressure. How did you notice it, in your body or in your thoughts?
- What do you usually do when stress rises? Does it help, or does it make things worse?
- Who can you talk to when a day has been too much?
