title: Practice
questions:
  - question: In a planning meeting, one volunteer talks for most of the time and two others say nothing. What would help the team most?
    options:
      - text: Stay quiet too, to avoid conflict.
      - text: Tell the talkative one to stop in front of everyone.
      - text: Keep your own turns short and ask the quiet members what they think.
        correct: true
      - text: Finish the meeting early, since the plan seems fine.
    explanation: A team decides better when all views are on the table. Inviting quiet members and keeping your own turns short makes room for them without embarrassing anyone. Silence or ending early leaves their ideas out, and a public reprimand can make the group more tense.
  - question: You finish your part of the festival set-up early, while your teammates are still working. What do you do?
    options:
      - text: Ask the team what is most useful and join in.
        correct: true
      - text: Take a break, since your part is done.
      - text: Redo your own part to make it perfect.
      - text: Start on a task you like, without telling anyone.
    explanation: Team work counts the result of the whole group, not only your own part. Asking what is most useful avoids doubling work or taking over a task someone is already doing. Resting or perfecting your own part may be fine later, but the team still has unfinished work.
  - question: Your team is deciding how to run a game with teenagers. You suggested one idea, but the group picked another. What is the best response?
    options:
      - text: Join in without effort, so everyone sees the choice was wrong.
      - text: Insist until the group changes its mind.
      - text: Leave the activity to the others.
      - text: Accept the decision, help it work, and share your concern at the review afterwards.
        correct: true
    explanation: A group decision needs everyone's support to succeed. Helping it work gives the team a fair test, and the review is the place to say what you noticed. Holding back effort or pressing on after the group has decided damages trust.
  - question: A teammate has not sent their part of a joint report and the deadline is tomorrow. What do you do first?
    options:
      - text: Complain about them in the group chat.
      - text: Write to them privately, ask if something is blocking them and offer to help or to redistribute the work.
        correct: true
      - text: Do their part without telling anyone.
      - text: Wait until tomorrow and then tell the supervisor.
    explanation: A private, friendly question gives the teammate a chance to explain and gives the team a chance to adjust while there is still time. Public complaints create defensiveness. Silently covering for them hides the problem, and waiting leaves no time to fix anything.
  - question: Your team's event gets praise from the coordinator, and you did a large share of the work. How do you respond?
    options:
      - text: Say that it went well because of your effort.
      - text: Thank the coordinator and name what other team members did.
        correct: true
      - text: Say nothing, to look modest.
    explanation: Teams keep working well when people feel their contribution is seen. Naming what others did gives credit where it belongs, and you can still accept thanks for your part. Taking all the credit hurts the team, and saying nothing leaves the praise unshared.
---

Read these situations and think about what you would do as a member of the team.

In an online campaign group, two people are working on the same graphic without knowing it. You find out on the day it is due.

At a refugee support centre, the shift lead changes the schedule at the last minute and half the team is annoyed.

In a student project, the person who always leads is on holiday for a week. Nobody knows who should decide.
