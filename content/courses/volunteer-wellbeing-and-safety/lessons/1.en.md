---
title: 'Looking after yourself'
---

Anke volunteers at a shelter for people without a home in Ghent. She says yes to every extra shift. After two months she sleeps badly, dreads Tuesdays and snaps at a friend. She tells herself that others have it worse.

Helping others matters, and it also takes energy. Tiredness that does not go away after a rest, feeling irritable or numb, losing interest in things you enjoy and dreading your shifts are common signs that you need a change.

## What helps

- Agree on a realistic schedule and keep rest days.
- Say no, or "not this week", without a long explanation.
- Talk to someone you trust, such as a friend, family member or your coordinator.
- Keep a small routine that has nothing to do with volunteering.

## When it is more than tiredness

If you feel unwell for a long time, or a project is affecting your mental or physical health, speak to your coordinator and to a qualified professional of your choice. This course cannot give medical advice, and no volunteering role asks you to put your health second.

## Think about it

- What are your own first signs that you are overloaded?
- Which limit would you set this month?
