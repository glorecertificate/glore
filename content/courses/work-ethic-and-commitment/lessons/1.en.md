---
title: Saturday at eight
---

Tomás volunteers every Saturday morning at a community kitchen in Seville. He joined in September and promised the coordinator, Carmen, that he would take the 8:00 sorting shift until Christmas. This Saturday it is raining, he has an exam on Monday, and two other volunteers have already written to say they cannot come.

He is tired and thinks about staying home. He also knows that the kitchen serves lunch to sixty people and that the food has to be sorted by ten. He decides to go, and on the way he writes to Carmen that he can stay only until noon because of his exam. She thanks him and asks another volunteer to cover the afternoon.

At the sorting table, Tomás finds a crate of yoghurts whose date is today. Nobody checks the crates after him, and a quick look would be enough to pass them. He reads each date properly and sets aside six that have expired. It takes twenty extra minutes.

When he leaves, Carmen tells him that the kitchen is lucky to have someone who comes and does the job properly, even on a rainy day. He says he mostly wanted to be honest about his limits.

Think about your own experience:

- Think of a time you kept a commitment even though you did not feel like it. What helped you?
- When did you do a task less carefully because nobody would notice? How did you feel afterwards?
- Think of a promise you could not keep. When did you tell the other person, and how did they react?
