---
title: What work ethic and commitment mean
---

This is the definition we use in this course:

> Ability to give steady and honest effort to a task or a group, and to keep one's commitments until the work is done well.

Responsibility is about owning what you did, including mistakes. Work ethic and commitment are about how you work and whether people can count on you before anything goes wrong.

## Steady and honest effort

Steady effort means you keep going at a reasonable pace, including through the dull parts. It does not mean working without rest. A person who gives full attention for two hours and then takes a break is showing more work ethic than someone who is present for six hours and distracted.

Honest effort means the quality of your work is the same whether or not someone is checking. At a food bank, Tomás reads every date even though nobody would notice a missed one. That is work ethic.

## Keeping commitments

A commitment is a promise to a person or a group: a shift, a deadline, a meeting. Keeping it makes you someone others can plan around. In a university group project, Amara is asked to write the introduction by Thursday. She finishes it on Wednesday, so the others can edit it before the deadline.

Sometimes a commitment cannot be kept: you are ill, or a family matter comes up. Commitment then means saying so as early as you can, telling the person what you can still do and helping to find a solution. A short message on Wednesday is better than silence on Saturday. At a warehouse job, a colleague who cannot come texts the supervisor the evening before and offers to swap a shift on the weekend.

## Saying yes with care

Commitment also means being careful with your promises. If you already have an exam, a job and a volunteering shift, adding another weekly activity may cause you to fail all of them. Saying "I can do it, but only from March" or "I can do half" is an honest answer. A smaller commitment that you keep is worth more than a large one that you drop.

## Finishing the work

Starting is easy, and finishing is where commitment shows. The last ten percent, such as cleaning up, checking the details or sending the final message, is often the part nobody sees. In a local sports club, a volunteer who organises a tournament also stays to count the equipment and writes a short thank-you note to the families. The next tournament is easier to organise because of it.
