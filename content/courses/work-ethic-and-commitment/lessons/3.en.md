title: Practice
questions:
  - question: You promised to cover a Saturday shift at the youth centre, and on Friday night you get a fever. What is the best response?
    options:
      - text: Go anyway and hide that you feel ill.
      - text: Say nothing and hope someone else covers for you.
      - text: Cancel on Saturday morning, because by then you will know for sure.
      - text: Message the coordinator as soon as you know, say what you can still do, and help to find a replacement.
        correct: true
    explanation: Commitment does not mean attending at any cost. It means giving the other person time to adapt. An early message and an offer to help turn a broken promise into a manageable change. Silence or a last-minute cancellation leaves others to cope alone.
  - question: You are counting donated items and nobody checks your work. You realise you miscounted a box an hour ago. What do you do?
    options:
      - text: Leave it, because the difference is small and nobody will notice.
      - text: Correct the count and tell the person who keeps the records.
        correct: true
      - text: Correct it silently and hope that no one asks.
      - text: Ask a colleague to say that they miscounted.
    explanation: Honest effort means your work is reliable even when unobserved. Telling the person who keeps the records means the numbers are right everywhere they are used. Correcting silently may leave others working from the old figure, and leaving it or shifting the blame keeps the error in place.
  - question: In a group project, a teammate says that the sources do not need to be checked because nobody reads them. What shows work ethic?
    options:
      - text: Check your own sources carefully and suggest that the group go through them together.
        correct: true
      - text: Skip the check to keep the group at the same pace.
      - text: Check all the sources and complain loudly that the rest are lazy.
      - text: Tell the teacher without talking to your teammates.
    explanation: Doing your part well, and inviting others to do the same, keeps the standard and the relationship. Skipping the check lowers the standard. Complaining or reporting without a conversation damages trust and does not improve the work.
  - question: You have to label 500 envelopes, and halfway through you are bored and tired. What helps you keep the quality?
    options:
      - text: Speed up and skip the ones with difficult addresses.
      - text: Stop until you feel motivated again.
      - text: Break the work into batches of fifty, take a short break after each, and check one envelope in each batch.
        correct: true
    explanation: Steady effort depends on small structures, not on motivation. Batches and short breaks keep your attention, and the quick check catches mistakes early. Rushing lowers quality, and waiting for motivation often means not finishing.
  - question: You have said yes to five activities this month and cannot do all of them well. What is the most committed response?
    options:
      - text: Do all of them half-heartedly so nobody is disappointed.
      - text: List your commitments, tell the people affected which ones you cannot keep, and focus on those you can do well.
        correct: true
      - text: Drop the ones that are hardest to explain without telling anyone.
      - text: Keep them all and complain to friends about how stressed you are.
    explanation: Being honest about limits early allows others to find alternatives, and it protects the quality of the commitments you keep. Doing everything badly or disappearing hurts everyone. Complaining does not change the situation.

Read these situations and think about what you would do.

At a beach clean-up you volunteered for, it rains lightly and half of the team does not show up.

You have a deadline tomorrow and a friend asks you to help with a presentation tonight.

At a summer job, your supervisor leaves early and says you can close the kitchen as you like.
