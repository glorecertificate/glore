---
title: 'What is GloRe?'
excerpt: 'GloRe turns what you learn while volunteering into a certificate you can add to your CV and anyone can check.'
---

GloRe, short for Global Recognition, helps you show what volunteering taught you. Maybe you helped run a youth exchange, worked a festival shift, or supported people who had just arrived in your city. A CV rarely has a line for what you learned there. GloRe gives you one.

## How it works in short

You take short online courses on soft skills such as teamwork, empathy or problem solving. Each course ends with a self-assessment, where you rate your own level and describe a real moment when you used the skill. Once you have finished enough courses and your volunteering was long enough, you ask for a certificate. A tutor from your organization reads your request and decides. If they approve it, GloRe creates a PDF and a public page with a QR code, so an employer or a school can check that it is real.

## Who is behind it

GloRe is run by [Associazione Joint](https://associazionejoint.org), a non-profit in Milan that works on youth mobility, European volunteering and non-formal education. It began as an Erasmus+ project and grew into a network of organizations in Europe and Latin America that issue certificates under shared quality standards. You never join GloRe on your own. You join through one of those organizations.

## What a certificate says

A certificate records where and when you volunteered, how many hours you gave, what you did, and the skills you showed, each with a level that a tutor confirmed. It is a checked record of your experience, not a diploma or an official qualification.

## Languages and cost

GloRe works in English, Spanish and Italian. It is free for volunteers in member organizations.
